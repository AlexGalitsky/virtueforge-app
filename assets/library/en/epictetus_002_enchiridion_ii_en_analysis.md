### Philosophical Context
This text is a continuation of the Enchiridion, an analytical work of the Epicurean corpus. It emphasizes the importance of acknowledging one's aspirations and aversions in relation to virtue.

### Essence
- Acknowledging one's aspiration means achieving what one aims for.
- Acknowledging one's aversion means not falling into what one avoids.
- One who fails to achieve the goal of their aspiration is unfortunate, and one who falls into what they avoid is miserable.
- Avoiding things that contradict nature among those within one's power means not falling into what one avoids.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Acknowledging aspirations and aversions is the key to achieving virtue. This means that recognizing what one aims for is the first step towards its attainment, and acknowledging what one avoids means not falling into what one seeks to avoid.

### Connection to Virtue
Acknowledging aspirations and aversions is a crucial aspect of forming virtue. This involves understanding what one aims for and what one avoids, as well as making decisions on how to act in these situations.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us truly achieves the goal of their aspiration, and who only falls into what they seek to avoid?
