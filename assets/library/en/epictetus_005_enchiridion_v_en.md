People are troubled by things themselves, not their own perceptions of these things. For instance, death is not frightening in itself, as it would have seemed so to Socrates if he had perceived it that way. However, since the opinion about death instills fear, it is the cause of fear. Thus, whenever we find ourselves facing difficulties, being troubled or sad, let us not blame anyone else but ourselves, namely our own opinions. The untrained person tends to blame others for their misfortunes. As one begins to cultivate oneself, blaming others becomes less characteristic. But for the well-trained individual, blaming either others or oneself is out of character.
