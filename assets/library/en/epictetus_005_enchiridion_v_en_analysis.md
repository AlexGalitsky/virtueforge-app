### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the Enchiridion, an autograph of Socrates written by his pupil Theodotus. It presents ethical and philosophical principles aimed at seeking virtue and happiness.

### Essence
- People encounter difficulties not due to the things themselves, but because of their own perceptions of these things.
- Our responsibility lies with our emotions and thoughts, not with external circumstances.
- A cultivated individual tends to blame themselves for their misfortunes rather than others.
- Therefore, we must recognize our role in creating our own reality.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This idea is linked to the concept of "self-opinion" (hupolepsis), which is a key aspect of Stoicism. According to this theory, each person should strive for their opinion to be accurate and just, rather than being subservient to the opinions of others.

### Question to Ponder
Is it always appropriate to blame oneself for one's misfortunes?
