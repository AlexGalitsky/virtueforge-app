### Philosophical Context
This passage from Enchiridion IX is part of the Stoic philosophical school, which emphasizes the importance of controlling one's thoughts and emotions. Stoics believed that through rational thinking and decision-making, a person could change their behavior and destiny.

### Essence
- The distinction between bodily and mental experiences.
- The possibility of overcoming obstacles through rational thought.
- The importance of self-reflection in each case.
- The ability to find a skill to overcome each situation.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtue of patience, as it underscores the importance of accepting hardship as a means to overcome obstacles.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us has not faced situations where we must choose between a rational and an emotional response?
