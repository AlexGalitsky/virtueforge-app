### Philosophical Context
In Enchiridion XII, Seneca emphasizes the importance of humility and renouncing desires for property and power. This text is part of a broader Stoic approach to life that prioritizes moral well-being over material prosperity.

### Essence
- Seneca recommends rejecting thoughts that generate anxiety and fear regarding one's possessions and relationships.
- He suggests beginning with minor issues, such as a leaky oil lamp or a stolen bottle of wine, to practice indifference and calmness.
- The aim is to cultivate inner strength and peace, rather than relying on external sources of comfort.
- By letting go of our attachment to material possessions and desires, we can achieve a state of freedom from worry and anxiety.

### Connection to Virtue
Renouncing desires for property and power is linked to the virtue of temperance and the ability to manage one's desires and thoughts.
