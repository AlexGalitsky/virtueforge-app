### Philosophical Context
This passage from Enchiridion XIV presents a Stoic philosophical thought on how one should properly relate to desires and desired events in their life. Stoics believe that a person should be equanimous and equal towards all people, including those beyond their control.

### Essence
- To not consider others as oneself, and oneself as others, requires the ability to distinguish between what is external and what is internal.
- Only the one who has power over their own desires and aversions can be the master of all.
- The value of freedom lies in not striving for what is within the power of others, but also not shying away from one's own desires.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: To be free, one must be able to distinguish between what is external and what is internal, and have control over one's own desires and aversions.

### Connection to Virtue
Stoic philosophy emphasizes the importance of equality and freedom, which are key aspects of virtue. While virtue is often associated with personal qualities, Stoic philosophy underscores the importance of inner balance and control over one's desires.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us truly is the master of their life, and who is merely a seeming slave to their own desires?
