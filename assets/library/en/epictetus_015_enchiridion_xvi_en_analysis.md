### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the Enchiridion, a philosophical work by Seneca written for young philosophers and politicians. It emphasizes the importance of restraining emotions and reasoning when interacting with others.

### Essence
- It highlights the necessity of distinguishing between external circumstances and a person's internal judgment, which can often be the primary cause of their sorrow.
- It advises against being swayed by the emotions of others and not assuming that their misfortunes are the result of external events.
- It is important to control one's emotions and refrain from crying with others, so as not to reveal that this is truly an issue with one's inner self.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is connected to the virtue of prudence (phronesis), which includes the ability to reason and understand complex situations, as well as the capacity to manage one's emotions and act with wisdom.

### Question to Ponder
