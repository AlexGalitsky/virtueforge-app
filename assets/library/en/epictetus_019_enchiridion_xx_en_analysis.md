### Philosophical Context
This text is attributed to Seneca, a Roman philosopher and thinker who was a prominent figure in Stoicism. In Enchiridion XX, Seneca emphasizes the importance of controlling one's emotions and thoughts to achieve inner strength.

### Essence
- Seneca asserts that our emotions and reactions to others are not real but rather reflections of our own opinions.
- He advises acknowledging when our thoughts and emotions lead to irritation and attempting to manage them.
- Seneca underscores the importance of rest and respite in achieving inner strength.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtue of humility, as it underscores the importance of controlling one's thoughts and emotions to achieve inner strength. This aligns with Franklin Weeks' Order, which also emphasizes the importance of humility and self-control in achieving inner strength.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us does not know someone who irritates us? But can it truly be that our emotions and reactions to others are not real but rather reflections of our own opinions?
