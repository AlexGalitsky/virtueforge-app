# The Gift of Moderation

§ 1. If someone has been given preference over you at a banquet or has been greeted before you, or if they have been chosen as advisors instead of you, and all these things are considered blessings, then rejoice that the person who received them is blessed. But if not, do not be disheartened that you did not receive them. Remember that you cannot deserve an equal share if you do not earn it by doing what others do, even though it's beyond your control.

§ 2. How can someone deserve an equal share with those who are doing something? Will they not be surpassed by those who surpass them? And will they not be envied by those who are envied? So, if you want to receive these blessings without paying the price, you will be unjust and greedy.

§ 3. But how much does one have to pay for a turnip? Perhaps it's just a small amount. So, if someone pays that amount and gets their turnip, while you don't pay anything, do not think that you are losing out on anything. You both have something: the person has their turnip, and you have your own something.

§ 4. The same is true here. If you were not invited to the banquet, it's because you did not give enough in return for the favor. This banquet is sold with praise and flattery. So, pay the price for this meal if you can benefit from it. And if you want to receive both the meal and the praise, without losing either one, then you will be unjust and foolish.

§ 5. You do not have anything in return for the meal? Then there's no need to praise those who are not worthy of your praise, and to tolerate their arrogance.
