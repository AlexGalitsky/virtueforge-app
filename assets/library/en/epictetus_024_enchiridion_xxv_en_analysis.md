### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the Enchiridion by Marcus Aurelius, a Roman philosopher and statesman, written in 150-180 AD. The text is dedicated to moral principles and practical advice for achieving virtue.

### Essence
* The possibility of receiving benefits does not necessarily mean one should rejoice or lament over them.
* Dignity and justice cannot be attained if what is required by justice is not done.
* Personal gratitude for achievements is not a necessary condition for virtue.
* The decision not to receive something does not imply that one has less than others.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: If you receive a benefit, rejoice, but do not think it is yours; if you do not, do not despair, for it was never yours to begin with.

### Connection to Virtue
In this text, the importance of fairness and equality in regard to benefits is emphasized. This aligns with the principles of virtue, which stress the necessity of actions and duties rather than personal gains.
