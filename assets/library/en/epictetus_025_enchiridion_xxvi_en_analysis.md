### Philosophical Context
This passage from Enchiridion XXVI is written in the Stoic tradition, emphasizing the importance of empathy and equality before the law. Stoics believed that every individual should live in harmony with nature and not succumb to emotional reactions.

### Essence
* Equality before the law: All people should be treated equally, regardless of their social status or personal circumstances.
* Empathy and compassion: When we witness the misfortune of others, we should feel the same sense of sorrow and remember that we too are capable of suffering.
* Consistency with nature: We should align ourselves with human nature and not contradict it, even in the most challenging situations.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: When we see the misfortune of others, we should feel the same sense of sorrow and remember that we too are capable of suffering, in order to align ourselves with human nature.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtues of honesty and justice. Stoics believed that each person should live in harmony with nature and not be swayed by emotional reactions, thereby demonstrating honesty and justice.

### Question to Ponder
Is it ever possible to remain indifferent to the misfortunes of others, or is there a place for empathy and emotional response?
