### Philosophical Context
In this text, Seneca emphasizes the idea that nature contains nothing inherently evil; all problems arise from human choice and action.

### Core Message
- *Natura non facit saltus*: Nature does not create evil.
- Suffering and evil are the result of human choice and actions.
- Humans are not born with a predisposition towards good or evil, but their behavior is shaped by their experiences and choices.
- Evil is the consequence of human misjudgment and inappropriate actions.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Human choice and actions are directly linked to the formation of virtues, as virtues are the result of proper and ethical human actions.

### Question to Ponder
What is the difference between the nature of evil and human choice?
