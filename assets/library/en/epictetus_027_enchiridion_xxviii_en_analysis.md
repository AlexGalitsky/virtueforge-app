### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the book "On Virtues" (Enchiridion) by Seneca, a student of Aristotle. In this passage, Seneca emphasizes the importance of internal conflict and contradictions that arise in the human mind when dealing with moral dilemmas.

### Essence
- Seneca poses the question of how a person reacts to an insult or injury from others when it pertains to their body but not their mind.
- He underscores the importance of inner tranquility and balance in moral decisions.
- Seneca asserts that a person must be capable of viewing themselves and their actions critically, rather than reacting emotionally.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Seneca's question about how a person reacts to an insult or injury from others when it pertains to their body but not their mind highlights the importance of inner tranquility and balance in moral decisions.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtue of justice (righteousness), as it underscores the importance of critical thinking and balance in making moral decisions.

### Question to Ponder
