Enchiridion XXIX
================

### § 1

When embarking on any endeavor, consider what leads to it and what is expected of it. Otherwise, you will hastily take on the task without careful consideration, only to abandon it in a humiliating manner when difficulties arise.

### § 2

If you aspire to compete at the Olympic Games, I too wish to do so, for it is a noble pursuit. However, examine what leads to this endeavor and what is required of it. You must adhere to discipline, maintain a prescribed diet, abstain from excesses, train in physical necessity at designated times, regardless of heat or cold, and limit your consumption of wine. In short, you will need to entrust yourself to the guidance of your mentor, just as a student would trust their physician. When the time for competition arrives, you must face it with courage, even if you suffer injury, such as breaking a limb or bruising your shins.

### § 3

By sound reasoning, if you still harbor the desire to train, proceed with your exercises. Otherwise, you will act like children who play at being athletes, gladiators, trumpet players, and actors, without committing fully to any one role. You will be a jester, mimicking everything you see, and finding it all appealing. This is because you undertook the endeavor without careful consideration, failing to examine its nature beforehand.

### § 4

Some individuals, upon seeing a philosopher or someone who speaks eloquently, wish to engage in philosophy themselves.

### § 5

Before pursuing any endeavor, consider what kind of pursuit it is and then familiarize yourself with your own nature. Ask yourself if you were born for this task, just as each person was born for their own unique calling.

### § 6

If you believe that by following this path you can also enjoy the pleasures of life, eat, drink, experience desire and aversion, and endure hardships such as separating from loved ones, working hard, tolerating the scorn of a slave, becoming a laughingstock to others, and sacrificing everything for fame, power, and glory, then consider whether you truly wish to adopt this state of being. If not, do not pursue it, lest you become like children who pretend to be something they are not.

### § 7

Reflect on whether you desire to cultivate inner freedom, serenity, or indifference. If not, then do not attempt to become one of these things, lest you become a pretender, a sophist, a rhetorician, or a procurator of the Caesar's court. You should strive to be a focused individual, either good or bad, and cultivate your rational faculty or focus on external matters that are unrelated to yourself. This means embracing either the role of a philosopher or an ordinary person.
