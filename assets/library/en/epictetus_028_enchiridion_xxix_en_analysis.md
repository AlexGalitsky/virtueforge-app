### Philosophical Context
This passage from Enchiridion XXIX, written by Seneca, the Roman philosopher and statesman, underscores the importance of considering the consequences of one's actions and choosing behavior that aligns with one's goals.

### Core Message
- It is crucial to contemplate the outcomes of one's actions and select those that align with one's objectives.
- Incorrect behavior can lead to dissatisfaction and a lack of direction.
- A philosopher must be a unified individual, maintaining a consistent stance rather than imitating everything they see.
- Actions should align with one's nature and goals.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Virtue is tied to the idea that a philosopher must be a cohesive individual, maintaining a consistent stance rather than imitating everything they see. This requires them to reflect on their goals and actions to ensure they align with their nature.

### Question to Ponder
How can I ensure that my actions align with my nature and goals?
