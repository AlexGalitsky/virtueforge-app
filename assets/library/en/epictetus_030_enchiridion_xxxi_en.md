Enchiridion XXXI
================

§ 1. Understand that the meaning of a virtuous relationship with the gods is to have an awareness that they exist and are perfect, and that they govern the world justly; that you are placed in a position to obey them, yield to what happens, and voluntarily follow them as if it were all part of a well-designed plan. For this way, you will not blame the gods nor accuse them of wrongdoing.

§ 2. It is impossible for this to occur otherwise than by denying the concept of good and evil in external things and transferring it exclusively to things within your power. So, if you consider something to be good or bad based on external circumstances, you will inevitably begin to criticize yourself and blame others for your failures, placing yourself in an undesirable position.

§ 3. All living beings are naturally inclined to avoid and repel anything that appears harmful, as well as its causes, and to pursue and admire everything beneficial and its causes. Therefore, it is impossible for someone who believes they are being harmed to be happy about their circumstances, just as harm itself cannot bring joy.

§ 4. From this, we can see why a father always hears complaints from his son because he does not give him a share in what appears good. For the opinion that tyranny is beneficial made enemies of Polynicus and Eteocles. As a result, the farmer complains about the gods, as do the shipwright, merchant, and those who have lost wives and children. Because where there is profit, there is also piety. So, anyone who takes care to pursue noble striving and avoidance is, in doing so, showing concern for piety.

§ 5. Making offerings and performing sacrifices, and sacrificing according to ancestral customs, is fitting for the cultivation of one's soul with the first fruits of the harvest, rather than lightly and without effort, or stingily, but also not beyond what is possible.
