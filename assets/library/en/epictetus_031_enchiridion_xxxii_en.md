§ 1. Every time you approach divination, remember that the outcome is unknown to you and that you are seeking it from the oracle. What is it if you are a philosopher? You will learn it when you arrive there. If the result is something from external circumstances, then it cannot be good or evil by necessity.

§ 2. Do not bring desires or reluctance to the oracle, but go to it without fear, understanding that everything that happens is indifferent and does not concern you. Whatever it may be, it can be used for your benefit, and no one will prevent it. So, strengthening your spirit, approach the gods as if they were advisors. And when you receive an answer, remember who you consulted and whom you will disobey if you do not submit.

§ 3. Approach the oracle in the manner of Socrates, only when consulting about the outcome of a matter and when neither reason nor art can provide the desired response. Do not ask the oracle whether or not to help a friend or family member in danger. If the oracle says that the sacrifices were unfavorable, it is clear that it refers to death, injury, or exile. However, good sense convinces even in such circumstances that helping a friend and sharing the risk with family are the right things to do. Therefore, direct your mind towards the greater oracle, Pheidippus, who expelled from his temple anyone who did not help a friend in a moment of mortal danger.
