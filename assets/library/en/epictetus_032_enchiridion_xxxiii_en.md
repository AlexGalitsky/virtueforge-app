**The Discourses XXXIII**

§ 1. Acquire for yourself a character and type that you can keep to yourself and with others.

§ 2. Mostly maintain silence. If necessary, speak briefly about the essential matters. Occasionally, when circumstances require it, speak, but not about trivial things like gladiators, horse racing, athletes, food, and drink, which are usually discussed. Especially avoid speaking ill or praise of people.

§ 3. Try to translate your conversation partners' speeches into a suitable topic if possible, and if you find yourself among strangers, remain silent.

§ 4. Do not laugh excessively about various things and do not let your laughter become uncontrolled.

§ 5. Do not swear unless absolutely necessary. If so, refrain from doing so as much as possible.

§ 6. Refuse to attend parties with people who are not familiar with wisdom. However, if you ever find yourself in such a situation, be careful not to fall into indecent behavior. Know that even if your friend is virtuous, you will inevitably become corrupted by their company.

§ 7. Take care of your body only as much as necessary, for example, food, drink, clothing, shelter, servants, and all else related to fame and pleasure. Discard everything that is not essential.

§ 8. When it comes to amorous affairs, try to maintain purity until marriage. If you have fallen into such temptations, take only what is lawful. However, do not be in the company of those who are devoted to such vices, do not expose them, and do not criticize them publicly if you are not among them.

§ 9. If someone tells you that you have spoken ill of someone, do not try to justify yourself. Instead, respond: "This person did not know about my other faults, and otherwise he would not have limited himself to these."

§ 10. There is no need to frequently attend spectacles. If ever the opportunity arises, show that you are not concerned with anything except yourself. This means that you want what happens to happen and for the one who wins to triumph. By doing so, you will not experience any difficulties. However, do not completely abstain from laughing, smiling, or making strong gestures, as these can give the wrong impression.

§ 11. Do not attend public readings lightly or without a thought. If it ever happens, maintain seriousness and composure while also being mindful of not offending anyone.

§ 12. Every time you are about to meet someone, especially if they are influential people, imagine that Socrates or Zeno would behave in this situation, and you will know how to act with dignity.

§ 13. When approaching someone influential, imagine that you will never see them again, that their door will not open for you, or that they will push you away. If it is fitting to do so, then arrive calmly and do not say to yourself that the situation was not worth it. This means acting like a commoner and being offended by external things.

§ 14. In conversations, do not often mention your troubles or misfortunes. The more pleasant it may be for you to recall something, the less pleasant it will be for others to listen to your stories.

§ 15. Do not have the desire to make people laugh. Such behavior is a slippery slope to foolishness and weakens the respect of those around you.

§ 16. Be careful not to fall into bad speech. Every time something like this happens, express your criticism if it is suitable, or remain silent, showing that you are upset by what was said.
