### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the Enchiridion, a book by the Stoic philosopher Marcus Aurelius, in which he offers guidance on living according to Stoic principles. It is particularly relevant to the theme of virtue and the importance of adhering to one's convictions.

### Essence
- Stoics believe in the importance of making decisions without judging others.
- It is crucial to be prepared for criticism and fate when we do what we consider right.
- We should focus on our beliefs and not succumb to public opinion.
- Stoics hold that proper behavior is the foundation of virtue.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Following one's beliefs and making decisions without judging others are key aspects of virtue in Stoic philosophy. This is because virtue entails consistent behavior based on moral principles.

### Question to Ponder
Why might a person who does what they believe to be right feel fear in the face of criticism or fate?
