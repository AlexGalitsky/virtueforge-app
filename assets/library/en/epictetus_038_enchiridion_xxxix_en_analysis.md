### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the Enchiridion by Epictetus, a philosophical work written in the 1st century BCE, dedicated to outlining the principles of Stoicism. This excerpt emphasizes the importance of moderation and restraint in life.

### Essence
- The measure of possessions for each individual is their body, implying that every possession should align with personal needs and capabilities.
- The principle of "measure" entails self-restraint to avoid excessive pleasure or surplus.
- If one exceeds the measure, they are more likely to suffer from the consequences (for example, shoes that are too large for the feet).
- This thought underscores the importance of moderation and balance in life.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This principle is linked to the virtue of humility (tapeinophrenia), which involves self-restraint and the avoidance of excessive pleasure or surplus. Humility is a key aspect of Stoicism, helping individuals remain balanced and restrained.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us has not exceeded the measure in their life, only to suffer the consequences?
