### Philosophical Context
This text is part of Seneca's *Enchiridion*, a guide to philosophy and morality written in the 1st-2nd century CE.

### Essence
* Inflicting harm is not a personal attack but rather a consequence of the perpetrator's actions.
* A person cannot adhere to another's beliefs if they do not align with their own convictions.
* If someone's beliefs are deemed false, the one who falls into error suffers, not the critic.
* Considering this, a person should be indifferent to their detractor and respond with, "It seemed so to him."

### Form of This Version
A prose paraphrase.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtue of honesty (integrity) and the ability to distinguish one's own beliefs from those of others.

### Question to Ponder
Who among people can be truly independent from their own beliefs?
