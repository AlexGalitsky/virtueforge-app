### Philosophical Context
This passage from Enchiridion XLIII exemplifies Stoic philosophy, emphasizing the importance of humility and balance in the face of injustice. This approach is reflected in the idea that we should view the matter not as a perfect injustice but rather as the act of a human brother, whom we have both nurtured together.

### Core Message
- We need to distinguish between two aspects of the matter: one for which we can act (for example, attempting to rectify the situation) and another for which we cannot bear to act (for example, being angry at our brother).
- By considering the matter from the perspective of a human brother, we can take control of ourselves and act humanely.
- This approach requires humility and the ability to discern different aspects of the situation.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is connected to the virtue of humility, as it underscores the importance of considering the human aspect of the matter, rather than its moral or legal significance alone. This requires us to be more sensitive and compassionate towards others.
