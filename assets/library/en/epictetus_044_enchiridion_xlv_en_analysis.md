### Philosophical Context
This text is part of Seneca's book "On Virtues," written in verse. In it, he emphasizes the importance of critical thinking and the analysis of one's own beliefs.

### Essence
- Critical examination of one's own opinions and beliefs.
- Uncertainty in evaluating the actions of others, as we cannot be sure of their motives and intentions.
- The impermissibility of accepting perceived images of things without critical analysis.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtue of honesty, as it underscores the importance of critical thinking and unwavering integrity in relation to one's own beliefs. It demands that we be honest with ourselves and not accept the opinions of others without scrutiny.

### Question to Ponder
Who among the people you frequently interact with might be right or wrong in their beliefs, but you cannot know for certain?
