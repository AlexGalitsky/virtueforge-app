### Philosophical Context
In this text, Seneca emphasizes the importance of moderation and self-discipline in relation to one's desires and needs. He urges a focus on inner well-being rather than external recognition.

### Core Message
- One's state of health should be a personal matter, not a subject for public boasting.
- Actions taken for cultural and physical benefit should be done solely for oneself, not for others.
- There is no need to draw attention to one's actions, even if they seem beneficial to others.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
This text is linked to the virtues of humility and self-discipline. Seneca underscores the importance of control over one's desires and actions rather than being ruled by them.

### Question to Ponder
Here is a question to provoke thought: "Can one be a virtuous person without seeking attention for oneself?"
