### Philosophical Context
This text is part of the book "Wisdom" (Enchiridion) by the Athenian philosopher Seneca. In it, he provides guidance on how individuals striving for wisdom and virtue should conduct themselves.

### Essence
- The ability to discern whether benefit or harm comes not from oneself but from external circumstances.
- Characteristics of one who excels in wisdom: avoiding criticism and praise of others, taking responsibility for obstacles, and laughing at those who praise or criticize.
- Eliminating desires and accepting the deviation from what contradicts nature.
- Utilizing a weakened desire and not concerning oneself with how it may appear.

### Connection to Virtue
These recommendations relate to virtue, as they imply the absence of criticism and praise towards others (honesty), taking responsibility for one's actions (diligence), and utilizing a weakened desire (forbearance).

### Question to Ponder
Who among the people you interact with might be an external impediment to your wisdom?
