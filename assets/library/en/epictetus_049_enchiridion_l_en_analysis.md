### Philosophical Context
This text is part of Seneca's book "Wisdom," written in the 1st-2nd century CE. It is one of the most renowned and influential works in Stoicism.

### Essence
* Let us regard all events and opinions as laws that we must obey.
* We should disregard the opinions of others, as they are not our own.
* We must retain everything we receive, regardless of its complexity or contradiction.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
These principles are linked to the virtues of honesty and independence. If we treat everything as laws, we must be prepared to follow them even when it means contradicting the opinions of others.

### Question to Ponder
Who among people can truly adhere to these principles without accountability to others?
