**The Discourse on Self-Discipline LII**

### § 1
When will you cease to delay counting yourself worthy of the best and not transgress the dictates of reason? You have received instructions with which you should have agreed, and you already concur with them. What more do you expect a mentor for, when you are no longer a youth but an adult man? If you become recklessly and frivolously delaying your self-improvement, setting new deadlines for yourself, and procrastinating to the point of never taking action, you will live out a life that is unfruitful in wisdom, and a mere simpleton.

### § 2
You already consider yourself worthy to live as a complete philosopher, and everything that seems best becomes an unshakeable law for you. Remember that when the time of your contest arrives, and the Olympic Games have begun, there is no more time to delay; success or failure will be decided in one day. 

### § 3
Socrates achieved perfection by focusing exclusively on the inner meaning of things. You should strive to live as a person who aims to become like Socrates, even if you are not Socrates himself.
