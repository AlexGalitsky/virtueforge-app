### Philosophical Context
This passage is part of the Enchiridion written by Seneca the Younger and focuses on the theme of overcoming delays and achieving perfection. The text emphasizes the importance of constant self-reflection and taking responsibility for one's actions.

### Essence
- The text underscores the importance of taking responsibility for one's actions and not postponing decisions.
- It stresses the necessity of continuous self-analysis and the pursuit of perfection.
- Socrates is highlighted as an example of a person who achieved perfection through focusing on the inner meaning of things.

### Connection to Virtue
Taking responsibility for one's actions and striving for perfection are linked to the virtues of honesty and justice, which are key aspects of Seneca's ethics.
