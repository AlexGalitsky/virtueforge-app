# Companion Analysis

This text comprises quotes and reflections by Epictetus, a Greek philosopher and student of Socrates. Currently, it is a compilation of ten rules he imparted to his students for living in accordance with human nature.

1. "True goodwill, if genuine and not feigned, is irresistible."
2. "The question arises: Shall we be mad or not?"
3. "Which would you prefer," Socrates asked, "to have the souls of rational beings or of irrational ones? 'Rational ones.' 'But corrupted or uncorrupted?' 'Uncorrupted.' 'Why, then, do you argue and dispute?'"
4. "No force can deprive us of the freedom to choose."
5. "He says it is necessary to establish rules that determine when to give consent. Regarding desires, it is essential to ensure they are not unconditional, are aimed at the common good, and are in accordance with dignity. From impulses, one should generally abstain, but reject nothing that is not within our power."
6. "The question arises: Shall we be mad or not?"
7. "Green, ripe, pressed—grapes change not into something that does not exist, but into something that now does not exist."
8. "When caressing your child," Epictetus said, "tell yourself: Perhaps tomorrow he will die."
9. "No force can deprive us of the freedom to choose."
10. "Which would you prefer," Socrates asked, "to have the souls of rational beings or of irrational ones? 'Rational ones.' 'But corrupted or uncorrupted?' 'Uncorrupted.' 'Why, then, do you argue and dispute?'"

These rules form the cornerstone of Epictetus's philosophy, emphasizing the importance of goodwill, freedom of choice, and empathy towards others.
