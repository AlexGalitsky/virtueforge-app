# The Twelfth Book

All that you wish to achieve through indirect means, you can have now if you only treat yourself with goodwill. That is, if you abandon the past, leave the future to providence, and yourself solely engage with the present, aligning it with piety and justice. With piety, to love your lot, for nature has adapted it to you and you to it. With justice, to speak the truth freely and without evasion and to act always according to law and in accordance with dignity, and to let nothing hinder you, neither another’s vice nor opinions nor gossip nor the sensations of the body that envelop you. Let the body that experiences them deal with them. Thus, if you, approaching the end, leave all else and honor only your guiding principle and the divine within you, if you fear not that life will someday cease but that it will never begin in accordance with nature, then you will become a person worthy of the world that produced you, no longer a stranger in your homeland, no longer marveling daily at what happens as if it were unexpected, and no longer dependent on this or that. God sees the guiding principle of each of us stripped of material coverings, bark, and excrescences. For God touches only what has proceeded and issued from him into the guiding principle, and only by his mind. If you habituate yourself to act similarly, you will free yourself from many troubles. Is not one who does not pay attention to the enveloping flesh and even less to clothing, dwelling, fame, and suchlike veils and adornments rich in leisure?

You consist of three parts: body, vital force, and spirit. Of these, the first two belong to you only insofar as you must care for them, while the third truly belongs to you. If you remove from yourself, that is, from your spirit, all that others do and say, or what you yourself did and said, all that troubles you as something that must be, which is beyond your choice, and which pertains to the body that envelops you or the vital force that is its birthright, all that the surrounding whirlwind drags you along with, and thus your spirit, freed from the milieu that fate has entangled it with, will be pure and free to order your life as you see fit, doing what is just, accepting what happens, and speaking the truth; if, I say, you remove from the guiding principle what has adhered to it from the pressure of passions and what pertains to the past or future, you will resemble Empedocles.

A sphere everywhere equal to itself, proud of its solitude, will think only of the life you are living, that is, of the present, and thus you will be able to spend the rest of your life peacefully, nobly, and in friendly communion with your genius.

I often marvel at how each person, loving oneself more than others, at the same time attributes less value to one’s own beliefs than to the beliefs of others. If, therefore, a god or a teacher filled with wisdom commanded someone not to think or reflect on anything about oneself that one would not immediately declare publicly, no one would endure even a single day. Thus, we are more concerned about our neighbors, what they might think of us, than about ourselves.

How did it come to pass that the gods, who have arranged everything so beautifully and with such love for humans, overlooked only the inconsistency that worthy people, as if having entered into a covenant with the divine principle and, through their pious deeds and holy lives, having become closely allied with it, after their death do not rise to a new life but fade away forever? Even if this is so, still be assured that if another order were to be established, they would have established it. For if it were just, it would also be possible, and if it were in accord with nature, nature would have produced it. From the fact that it does not exist (if it does not exist at all), you can conclude with certainty that it should not have come to pass. For you yourself notice that, raising such questions, you appeal to the justice of the gods; but you could not appeal to them in this way if they were not the best and supremely just. But in that case, they could not have neglected any oversight in the cosmic order against reason and justice.

Accustom yourself also to what seems to you impossible. For the left hand, generally speaking, is weaker than the right, but it holds the reins more firmly, for it is accustomed to it.

Think about the state of your body and soul when death overtakes you, about the brevity of life, about the yawning chasm of eternity before and after you, about the powerlessness of all that is material.

Consider the causal principles of things stripped of all excrescences and their purposes in actions. Think about what suffering is, what pleasure, what death, what fame, about how every person is the cause of their own turmoil, about how one person cannot hinder another, about how everything is based on belief.

Using your principles, you should be like a boxer, not a gladiator. For the latter, if he loses his sword with which he fights, is finished, whereas the former always has his fists at hand, and he needs only to clench them.

You must understand the properties of things themselves, distinguishing between matter, causal principle, and purpose.

How great is the power of a human: he can do nothing but what deserves praise from the gods and welcome all that the gods send him. For all this is in accordance with nature.

Do not blame the gods, for they err neither voluntarily nor involuntarily, nor do you blame humans, for they err involuntarily. Therefore, you need not blame anyone.

How ridiculous and ignorant is the person who marvels at anything that happens in life!

Either fatal necessity and unalterable order, or beneficent providence, or else a chaotic, uncontrolled confusion. If fatal necessity, then why do you resist? If beneficent providence, accessible to entreaty, then make yourself worthy of divine help. If, however, uncontrolled confusion, then be content already with the fact that among this whirlwind you yourself possess a certain guiding principle of spirit. And if the whirlwind carries you away, let it carry away only the body, vital force, and so on, for it will not carry away the mind.

The light of a lamp is bright and does not lose its brilliance until it is extinguished. Should truth, justice, and wisdom in you also fade before your death?

If someone causes in you the idea of a mistake they have made, say to yourself: “How do I know that this is a mistake? Or if it is a mistake, has he not already punished himself, like one who has plucked out his own eyes?”

He who does not want evil people to err is like one who does not want the fig tree to draw its sap from itself, children to cry, a horse to neigh, or anything else necessary to happen. What should a person of this sort do? If you have the courage, try to heal him.

If this is not fitting, do not do it; if it is a lie, do not speak it. Your aim should always be to discern what is in itself the thing that excites an idea in you and to investigate it, distinguishing in it the causal principle and the material principle, the purpose and the time within which it is limited.

Finally, understand that within yourself you have something more perfect and divine than that which arouses passion or generally attracts you. What now fills my soul? Is it not fear, suspicion, desire, or something else of this sort?

First, do not act without purpose and aim. Second, act in accordance with only one purpose, the common good, and with nothing else.

A little more time, and you will disappear, as will everything you see and all those who live now. For all is subject to change, transformation, and disappearance so that something else may arise in its place.

Everything is based on belief; it depends on you. Therefore, remove the belief when you wish, and, like a sailor who has rounded the rocks, you will find calm, smooth waters and a quiet harbor.

Any individual activity, when it ceases at the appointed time, does no harm from the cessation itself, nor does the person engaged in it suffer harm from its cessation. Similarly, the aggregate of activities called life, if it ceases at the appointed time, does no harm from the cessation itself, nor does the person who ends it in time suffer harm. Time and the appointed time are indicated by nature, sometimes by the nature of each individual, as in old age, and in all cases by the nature of the Whole, the changes of whose parts maintain the world in eternal youth and bloom. Everything that is beneficial to the Whole is always beautiful and timely. Therefore, the cessation of life is not evil for anyone, unless it is shameful, if it does not depend on our choice and does not go against the requirements of society; but it is a good if it is timely for the Whole, beneficial to it, and in its movement is in harmony with it. Therefore, the god’s companion is he whom the god moves along his paths, and his own reason is directed to divine ends.

Always have at hand the following three positions. Regarding your actions, remember that you should not act either in vain or otherwise than as justice would act. Regarding what happens outside you, that it owes its existence either to chance or providence. But do not complain about chance or grumble about providence. Second, observe the growth of each being from the germ to animation, and from animation to the separation of the soul, and take account of what it is made of and into what it is resolved. Third, know that if you were suddenly to rise above the earth and cast a glance at human affairs and their many changes, you would be filled with contempt for them, while at the same time you would have the opportunity to behold so many beings dwelling around you, in the air and ether, and that, however often you might rise in this way, you would always see the same, uniform, and fleeting. And this is what we glory in!

Abandon belief, and you are saved. But who can prevent you from abandoning it?

If you are indignant about something, you have forgotten that all is done according to the nature of the Whole, that another’s error does not concern you, that all has always been done in this way, is being done now, and will be done everywhere, and that the individual human being is related to the whole human race, not by blood and common origin, but by spirit. You have also forgotten that the spirit of each is a god and that nothing belongs to anyone inalienably, but that even his child, his body, and his very soul are taken from the same source, that everything is based on belief, and that each lives exclusively in the present and can lose only that. Constantly recall those who were given to excessive indignation over some matter, or who surpassed all in fame, fortune, enmity, or who were showered with gifts by fate. Then ask yourself: “Where is all this now?” Smoke, dust, and myth or even not myth. Also think about all that can be illustrated by Fabius Catullinus in his country estate, Lucius Lupus in his gardens, Stertinus in Baiae, Tiberius on Capri, Vellius Rufus, and generally about the prejudice connected with self-esteem, the insignificance of all objects of desire, and how much more worthy it is for a philosopher to exhibit justice, wisdom, and unassuming obedience to the gods on the material provided to him. Vanity, however, without vanity is the most intolerable form of vanity.

To those who ask: “Where have you seen the gods or from where did you learn of their existence that you so zealously revere them?” answer: “First, the gods are visible. Moreover, I have never seen my soul, and yet I honor it. So it is also with the gods: experiencing their power constantly, I have learned of their existence from this and bow to them.”

The salvation of life lies in investigating, regarding each thing, what it is in itself as a whole, what in it is from the material principle, what from the causal principle, and dedicating oneself entirely to the realization of justice and speaking the truth. What then remains but to enjoy life, linking one good deed with another so that not even the shortest interval remains?

The sun’s light is one, though it is broken by walls, mountains, and countless other objects. The common essence is one, though it is divided among countless individual and distinct bodies. The soul is one, though it is divided among many beings and distinct formations. The rational soul is one, though it seems divided. Other accompanying parts, such as vital forces and material principles, are insensible and alien to each other; yet they are held in unity by the rational principle and their own inertia. It is characteristic of reason to have a special affinity for what is akin to it, to draw near to it, and this impulse toward communion cannot be separated from it.

What are you seeking? A long life? In other words, you wish to receive sensations, to strive, to develop, and to regress, to use speech, to think? But what of all this seems worthy of desire? If all is contemptible, then move to the last: obedience to reason and god. But reverence for them is incompatible with indignation at the thought that death will deprive us of all the above.

What portion of the boundless and infinite time is allotted to each of us? Soon it will disappear into eternity. And what portion of the whole essence? Of the world soul? On what patch of earth do we crawl? Reflecting on all this, do not consider important anything but this: to act as your nature prescribes and to be content with whatever the common nature creates.

How does the guiding principle use itself? For in this lies everything. The rest depends on your choice or does not depend: dust and smoke.

Nothing inclines so much to the contempt of death as the thought that even those who considered pleasure a good and suffering an evil also despised death.

To a person for whom the good is only what is timely, for whom it is all the same whether to perform a greater or lesser number of actions in accord with right reason, and for whom it is indifferent whether to contemplate the world for a longer or shorter time, such a person is not afraid of death.

“Man, you were a citizen of this great City. Is it not all the same to you whether five years or three years? For obedience to the laws is the same for all. What then is terrible if it is not a tyrant or an unjust judge but the very nature that placed you there that sends you out of the City?” “But I have performed not five acts but only three.” “Quite right. But three acts are the whole play in life. For the end is compensated by the one who was once the cause of the creation of life and is now the cause of its cessation. You are not responsible in either case. Depart from life with goodwill, as the one who sends you is well-wishing.”
