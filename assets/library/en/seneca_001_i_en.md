To Lucilius, greetings from Seneca.

Only time is worth preserving.

Do not allow it to be stolen by moments of indolence,

By empty instants spent in pointless gatherings.

We spend our lives engaged in affairs, but not

Profitable ones, for the most part, but base...

Then comes idleness, and we carve out no time

For the remainder on other days.

You would name some acquaintance

Who knows that each hour he dies?

For death is not a dreadful slumber to come,

But present in each day, even now.

All else is foreign; only time is ours!

Yet we do not cherish it at all:

Every acquaintance proffers a cup,

And we fill it but "to the brim."

I strive to avoid lengthy letters

(Why recount trivial matters therein):

The less you depend on "tomorrow,"

The better you control your present day.

I marvel at how foolish people are,

And how futile their path is...

They count every coin when lending,

Yet no one returns the time they squander...

Those upon whom you expend a whole day

Do not sense that they are indebted!

Try to summon them all for repayment:

Each will reply: "Forgive me, I cannot!"

I am a spendthrift, meticulous in my calculations.

I know with whom and how much I have lost...

For time demands more careful accounting

Than any popular yellow mineral.

Only he is wealthy who finds contentment in little,

Who does not call upon physicians,

Uncomprehending of his own malady...

Remember time.

Be well.
