This poetic rendition captures the essence of the very first letter from the renowned series "Moral Letters to Lucilius," penned by the Roman Stoic philosopher Seneca in the first century AD.
## The Essence of the Work
In the original prose text, Seneca contemplates the paramount value of time for humans. The poet has accurately conveyed the philosopher's key ideas:

* Time is our sole possession. Everything else in the world is not truly ours.
* We die a little each day. Death is not an event in the future; the hours that have passed are already claimed by death.
* People undervalue others' time. When someone borrows money (in the poem, rubles), they understand the need to repay it. Yet, when people spend others' time, they do not feel obligated to return it, though this resource cannot be reclaimed.
* Mastery over the present. Seneca urges us to live each day to the fullest, thereby reducing our dependence on tomorrow.

## Unique Features of This Version
This is not a literal translation but rather an artistic poetic interpretation (paraphrase). The translator has adapted the text for a contemporary audience, employing rhyme, the pentameter iambic form, and accessible metaphors (such as "yellow mineral" for gold and "ruble" for ancient Roman coins).
