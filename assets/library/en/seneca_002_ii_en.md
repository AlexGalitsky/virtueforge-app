# The Letter II (On Changes)

Dear Seneca,

You write that you are calm, not troubled by the journey.

This is a human feeling, worth noticing with joy.

Do not worry about changes, for they are burdens on our souls.

Resting in one's established nature is better than rushing to nowhere.

Do not be swayed by fleeting fashions and books,

Read the greats, and let their thoughts sink deep into your mind.

The soul does not need superficial appearances,

But rather oxygen - the foundation of the earth.

Health is harmed by frequent changes in medication.

And neglecting food, as well as sleep...

A stationary plant, a human being, and a state do not stand firm with change.

Of course, everyone wants diversity,

But different stomachs cannot accommodate it all.

So return to the classics - their phrase

Is deeper than any superfluous style.

For those who are recognized as classics,

Are those who have achieved perfection in their craft.

What they said is more profound than any prelude.

You can transform it into a beautiful poem.

I am modest, but I read Epicurus,

Who called poverty a joyful thing.

But many see only drunkenness,

And celebrate his life like a carnival.

Do not be fooled by wealth that is hard to attain,

For it pushes you to cross this river.

But who avoids excess and does not want more,

Is truly healthy.

Be well.
