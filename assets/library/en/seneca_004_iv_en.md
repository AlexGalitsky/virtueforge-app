# Letter IV (On Fear of Death)

Lucillia greets Seneca!

What you begin, do. Hurry.

Forget the virtues: luxury, laziness, and cowardice,
You will reap a worthy harvest:

You will experience joy,
By embracing serenity in your soul.

Its stillness is not a vision of death,
But rather the chaos that lies within it.

Youth's recklessness - a sin of the elderly,
And childishness - their thoughtless folly.

Empty and futile are your fears. Death reigns supreme

One must pay the debt, which is owed to all.
Only by taking action can you understand: it's not as terrifying,

Death cannot stay with you forever.

It has come and become yesterday's day,
What's the point of fighting against it?

Someone else would rather die from fear...

Out of petty, insignificant causes.

They are willing to end their life with a single stroke.

In disdain for life - beauty lies.

Look at life calmly, serenely.

I ask you to do this in every moment.

Only those who leave behind their worries,
Will find courage and see the light.

Life becomes enjoyable,
And thoughts of God become clear.

A young boy killed Pompey's servant,
Caesar condemned Gaius Lepidus...

Even Fortune can lift us up or put us down.

Do not fear death, which is a source of power,
Even if you are weak in its face.

It's not just the danger that lies within it:
Even a slave can kill you.

If you have been sentenced to death,

You were born for it.

Only those who remember this,
Will walk their path without falling victim to fear.

What is the meaning of wealth, given by nature?

Not to be constantly hungry, cold, or drunk.

It's accessible to everyone.

Why do we seek something greater than that?

To wander aimlessly in a white world,
And to trample on the boundaries of moderation?

A person is rich who is content with life,

Who does not curse poverty.

Be healthy.
