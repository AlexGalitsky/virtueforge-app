# Letter VI (On the Transmission of Knowledge)

Dear Seneca,

I am constantly changing and improving myself. However, I see more distractions in my mind than ever before. My soul is crying out in tears.

I will not boast about my successes, for it is enough that my laziness decreases. We should also congratulate ourselves on noticing our own illness in the mirror.

I wish these changes could be shared with you... But how can we do so?

Our friendship must overcome fear, greed, and death. The success of my soul is so precious to me that I can only share it with others.

Even beneficial knowledge has no use if it's not cherished for oneself. I will send you books and notes, so you can see the value in them without effort.

Our life together was bright and sweet, but now we only meet occasionally.

The path of spiritual guidance is long and difficult. A single example is more convincing than a multitude of definitions.

Plato and Aristotle were happy to find their own spiritual father. I have found my inspiration in Socrates' wisdom, which is more profound than any statement from himself.

Today, I read at Hecaton: "I have become a friend to myself." This means living in harmony with oneself, without the need for external support or solitude.

Those who understand this concept will become friends with many. They will know the secret of the uncharted paths.

On the other hand, those who are alone will always be trapped in their own darkness.

May you stay healthy.
