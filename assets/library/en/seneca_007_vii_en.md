# The Crowd
---
Lucilius greets Seneca!

"Should we avoid crowds?" 

I agree that it is wise to steer clear of the masses,

For in such throngs, there is great danger,

That you will not return as you were before,

And instead of clarity of mind, you will be beset by confusion,

By vice, temptation, and unquenchable passion.

To me, they seem like a plague:

In them, I see cruelty, luxury, and sin,

A leader who guides people to indulge in pleasure,

And overcomes modesty with ease.

We must keep our distance from the crowd,

With a soul that is not swayed by goodness:

Otherwise, you will become a beast,

Striving only for gold.

The philosopher's ability to foresee,

Is this: you will either imitate or despise

The crowd, which forms a common circle.

There is a third path, though it may be complex,

Between the Scylla and Charybdis of excess,

Interact with those who can help you grow,

And become better together. In this, life lies.

Read the crowd's poetry with naive enthusiasm,

Be attentive to your own desires' cravings

(For we are all tainted by these vices),

But is there anyone who can understand?!

Perhaps just a few individuals...

With clarity?

"Then, for what did I study and love?" 

The answer is simple, and there is no other opinion:

I studied for myself.

But today, I studied for you...

And discovered three thoughts that occurred to me

(If fate had intervened)

Firstly, as Democritus wrote:

"The human being is akin to the crowd,

The crowd, in turn, is like a single person."

Another (who was skilled in art),

Said: "Let my labor be clear to few,

Let it be understood by only one,

I am confident that I am perfect,

Even if there were no one else."

Thirdly, Epicurus, who scorned the fleeting:

"Speak to you alone, not to a crowd.

You are like an empty arena,

Ready to listen and respond."

Do not be swayed by the fullness of the hall,

Do not heed flattery, spoken with cunning,

Your noble soul's worth is in your beginnings,

Which are far more valuable than gold.

Be healthy.
