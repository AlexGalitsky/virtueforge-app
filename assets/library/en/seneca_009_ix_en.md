# The Art of Acquiring Friends

Dear Socrates,

Stilpon said that only the wise
do not seek friendship in a worthless person,
for they find the beginnings and ends within themselves.
A wise man does not need a friend, for he is complete without one.
Alone with himself, like a god in church,
he feels neither sorrow nor poverty.

Whoever is wise remains calm at any loss.
And, indeed, he will always find a friend,
like Phidias, who restored his statue to its former glory
without much effort.

Acquiring friends is not as difficult as it seems,
as Hecatomnus once said:
"Love others if you want to be loved!"
There are those who seek friendship for the sake of being supported,
but only on another's shoulders will they find their burden.
Once they have lost that friend, they are left with nothing.

While in the midst of fame, one sees a vast ocean
of friends accompanying them,
but when misfortune strikes, one is left with an empty desert.
Some run away from danger out of fear,
while others betray their friends for the same reason.
All these things are like dust,
for those who have gained friendship through self-interest.

My friend is my brother, and I would gladly give my life
for him. Friendship that comes from selfishness is just a transaction,
and one should not cling to it.

Love can be stronger than reason,
with its passion, desire, and beauty.
It brings hope for an answer, even if it's not immediate,
not like the pursuit of profit.
A wise man values nothing more than wisdom,
although he needs many things.
A fool, on the other hand, is unable to appreciate
the value of anything, nor does he have any desires.

A wise man is willing to gather friends under his banner,
even if it means sacrificing some comforts.
For what greater blessing can there be?
When Hecatomnus said: "Love others if you want to be loved!"
He was right. A true friend is one who values the other person's well-being above all else.

When the city of Stilpon was burned by his enemies,
and he emerged from the ashes, Demetrius asked him:
"What have you lost?"
Stilpon replied: "Myself, for I am my own master."
This is a true Stoic, resolute and fearless.
There is no greater good than having the strength to overcome adversity.

The ideal is to strive for this kind of strength,
for it is the source of great power.
Only a few have been able to achieve this,
one of whom was Stilpon himself.
He embodied wisdom and greatness that will last forever.
Those who are foolish will not appreciate their blessings,
and they only need a little warmth to be content.

May one wise person bring happiness to many,
not just through words, but through actions.
