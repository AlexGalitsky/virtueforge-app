### Philosophical Context
This text is a pseudo-epistolary piece where Seneca offers advice and wise thoughts to his daughter, Lucilia. In the letter, he emphasizes the importance of striving for virtue and seeking examples of goodness in others.

### Essence
- Seneca underscores the significance of a ruddy complexion as a sign of youthfulness and dignity.
- He asserts that beauty is a natural result of well-being and health.
- Seneca advises Lucilia to aspire to a spiritual father and to emulate goodness.
- He stresses the importance of seeking someone who can praise her and being praised in return.

### Form of This Version
Prose

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca links beauty to dignity and well-being, reminiscent of Franklin's idea that "good manners are the first impression."

### Question to Ponder
Who among the people we can praise might serve as our spiritual father?
