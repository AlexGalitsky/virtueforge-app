### Philosophical Context
This text is part of a series of letters by Seneca, in which he discusses philosophical and moral issues with Lucilius. In this letter, Seneca addresses the theme of anticipating death and its impact on life.

### Essence
- Death is a natural consequence of life.
- Old age is a part of human existence and has its own advantages.
- Life is worth living with purpose and satisfaction.
- Time is available to us, and we should use it to achieve our goals and satisfy our needs.
- Immortality is a delusion, but life has meaning only in the sense that it allows us to enjoy its final fruits.

### Form of This Version
prose

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca emphasizes the importance of using life to achieve goals and satisfy needs. This aligns with the principles of virtue, which stress the importance of moral behavior and making decisions that promote the well-being of others.

### Question to Ponder
Are we fully prepared to recognize the value of our lives and use them to achieve our goals without expecting immortality?
