# The Letter XIII (On Courage)

Lucillia greets Seneca!

Let us discuss courage with you.

An athlete who has not bled,
Cannot emerge from adversity with bravery.

When troubles pursue you,
You, like a plowman on a field,
Show your strength, for we all know
That courage does not tolerate chance.

A blow from fortune, instead of its reward,
You often feel beneath yourself...

But, nobility increases when the challenge
Is thrown by an unyielding fate.

We are less troubled by what troubles cost
Than by speculations about what may be.

And this says to you: do not try to flee
From pain, for it is weak...

To a Stoic, you are insignificant.
But if you can endure, then perhaps you will deserve
A kingdom that lies beyond death.

Do not worry about the future,
For it has its own time.

When they tell you that you are unfortunate,
And weep over your misfortune,
Try to break down the trouble into parts:
Perhaps everything is nothing.

You ask: "Is my anxiety unnecessary?
Should I dig beforehand?"

Healthy? Free? - Then all is well.
But when will you see the future?

Do not be swayed by the crowd's opinion,
Even if it is popular.

Throw your doubts onto the scales of justice:
All things have their measure, and that measure is themselves.

Fear is weak - like life without faith or wisdom.
When pain comes, you will suffer...
Why do you flee from it?
You are punishing yourself twice,
What could have been avoided.

Sometimes fortune misses its mark
(Not always does she hit the target),
And the blow that was meant to strike you
Falls short, causing no harm.

When adversity looms like a dreadful fate,
Find strength in yourself.
Try to overcome your weakness with virtue,
Even if you ask for help from hope...

If you see the balance of hopes and fears as equal,
And cannot find the right line,
Then trust in hopes - they are true,
And have faith, for that is what you must do.

I am ashamed of such weak remedies...
Let even death bring an end to your life...
Perhaps, beyond it, you will see a kingdom
That you can deserve with courage.
