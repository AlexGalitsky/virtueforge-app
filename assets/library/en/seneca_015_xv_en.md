# Letter XV (On Health)

Lucilius greets Seneca,

"Since you are healthy, it is excellent," they have long written in Greek letters.

Philosophers are more reliable than doctors.

Health is in philosophy, not in the body...

Without it, your soul is ill.

No matter how many times we turn the wheel,

These muscles are worth nothing.

When we exhaust all our strength in training,

In our minds there is only one thing: to rest and recover...

There is no strength or skill in reason,

And even the finest subjects cannot be grasped.

Choose any exercises you like,

So that your body tires, but your character hardens.

I recommend quick movements,

And... do not forget to count time.

The less we look at time, the faster it passes,

And it will soon depart from us.

Take care of the happiness that comes with old age,

Only then will it strengthen, like fine wine.

I do not advise spending all day sitting and reading,

For your soul must rest.

If you prefer to take a break,

I recommend leisurely conversations and walks at your own pace.

Do not listen to those who want to teach you deceitful ways,

So that they can lead you astray...

Their audacity raises my suspicions,

That they have been fed by hunger for knowledge.

When you deliver a long speech,

Please pay attention to your voice, too.

So that, without losing control, it becomes softer and more gentle...

A life of fools is one of sadness, but also of fear:

"Tomorrow's all the worries."

Trying to outdo everyone with one swift move,

You are not even catching up with yourself...

Set limits for your desires,

And leave them empty.

For what you can truly achieve is understanding souls and the world's beauty.

This achievement is more important than all the feasts in gold.

There is Truth: always strive for it,

And do not copy wealth.

Be healthy.
