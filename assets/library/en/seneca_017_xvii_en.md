# Letter XVII (On Self-Denial)

Dear Seneca,

Are you troubled by household chores?

You strive to fill every corner,
not understanding that there is a limit...

While you only see the bigger picture,
remember that Cicero says:

That wisdom and greatness are saved for us in the large,
and modesty is granted to us in the small.

Wealth has hindered many from achieving philosophy,

while poverty is enough for life.

For the poor, it's not the true danger:
it's when the world around them cries out in floodwaters,

they think of how to escape themselves.

No army of slaves - no need for money

to feed them, but there is room for the mind.

Desiring freedom from the soul,
be poor and self-denying - it makes no difference.

Know this: whatever philosophy wants,

is contained within your own heart.

Don't speak with sorrow, lamenting:

I need this and that... How can I look at life?

Is it not the good things that trouble you?

And what troubles a warrior is not to be feared?

Who has been overcome will endure anything:

the endurance of the soul silences anger.

You will be rewarded - being free forever,

when your anger towards people and gods is no longer feared.

Warriors go on all sorts of adventures,

eating roots, withering away from water,

in the hope of conquering a kingdom.

But not for themselves... Without end.

The path to philosophy is known,

and money is not needed for it.

Why then leave the last resource?

That which we should seek salvation in.

Look into wisdom - its most important beginning,

perhaps you have lost your pouches.

And if you've lost your pouches,
then seek wisdom first.

"Riches are not the end of all our troubles,
they're just a sign of an unknown trouble."

(An ancient Persian king's secret to me:

I must bid you farewell without gold.)

Is there any difference between lying on a comfortable bed,

or on a bundle of twigs?

The soul, which is sick, reaches for something bad.

It's always with it.

Be healthy.
