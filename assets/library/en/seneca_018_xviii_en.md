# The Letter XVIII (On Fasting)

Lucilia greets Seneca,

The sound of Saturnalia echoes around,
The city is in a frenzy, with laughter explosions...

Is this joy for us, dear friend?

In such times, with a stern heart,
One sees: her firmness is high.

Do not be swayed by fleeting joy

Townspeople, laughing in masks.

When the crowd cries out from drunkenness,
Will they be distinguished among them?

They do the same thing, but differently,

For holidays are not just for pigs.

Try, as advised by Epicurus,

To follow a diet day by day,

And cover your figure with stern clothing...

You will say: "What's to fear in that?"

Soldiers are hardened before battle,
So they can show their bravery in the fight.

Who knew hardship, is already at peace,

And does not fear his fate.

You slept two or three days in a simple bed,

Or worse - was rocked by a deranged pillow?

When fortune will be our nemesis,

And we will be angry with her...

Do not think that you are doing something special.

You are engaged in an exercise like this.

Such is the lot of many thousands of poor people.

I remind you, my teacher of pleasures,

Could have lived without spending a penny on food...

Who has enjoyed within limits,

Will suffer and endure hardship when given the chance.

In prison, the condemned are fed abundantly,

And even those who should be executed...

Do not fear being defeated by fortune:

Who is hardened will deflect blows.

Worthy of God, raised from dust,

When his wealth is abundant.

Possess wealth, but possess it without fear,

All things are transitory: fame and honor.

Know this: "Strong anger ends in madness."

Only when you burn, will the firewood be consumed...

Let your reason signal like a zither:

Do not worry.

Be healthy.
