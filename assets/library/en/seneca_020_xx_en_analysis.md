### Philosophical Context
This text is a pseudo-epistolary piece written by Seneca, a Stoic philosopher, addressed to his friend Lucilius. In it, he emphasizes the importance of the unity of word and deed in achieving wisdom.

### Core Message
- Word and deed should be one unit.
- Wisdom is demonstrated in the congruence of speech and action.
- Few can attain such a level of wisdom, as most people are uncertain about their desires and often contradictory.
- Poverty can be a blessing if it prompts self-truthfulness and love.
- Spiritual value surpasses material wealth.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Poverty can be a blessing if it prompts self-truthfulness and love. This is because most people are uncertain about their desires and often contradictory, and poverty can serve as a means for their inner transformation.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us truly loves themselves and their life, or are we merely trying to satisfy our external needs?
