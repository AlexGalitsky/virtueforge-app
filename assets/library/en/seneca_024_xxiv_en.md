# The Courage of Manhood

Dear Seneca,

Is your stern judge looming over you?

You seek solace from a colleague:

"Trust in the best, hope for... these trifles"

I shall not utter them now.

To what purpose do we trample on worn-out roads?

There is another path to serenity, as I shall show,

Free from the turmoil of anxious thoughts.

Imagine that all your troubles have passed,

Weigh your misfortune and measure the crushing fear.

Then you will realize: your misfortune is a blessing:

Not long nor brief, like a collapse.

I can find many examples of such events,

Given by any past century:

Exile, torture, execution... the highest penalty

Is meted out to those who have broken the law.

Rutilius Rufus, trained in Pannaeum,

Has learned to condemn with calmness and composure.

The judges who are unjust are short-lived and rare.

Rufus awakened Sulla from his sweet dreams.

Socrates, who conversed in prison,

Was offered a chance of escape.

He refused, so as to demonstrate his courage

And show that there is success beyond fear.

Cato, who could not kill Porcena,

Sent himself into the fiery furnace:

Thus he declared his greatness.

Porcena was amazed and lifted the siege...

You will say: "These songs are mere echoes,

Will you tell me how Katon died?"

Yes, I shall... Let these two couplets

Teach future generations about it.

He did not fight for his freedom,

But for his country, for all hearts' liberty.

With one hand he held Plato's philosophy,

With the other, the sword - salvation and victory.

He fell on the sword... The doctors bound him,

To prolong his journey to the nearby grave.

But his hands were torn apart by the wound,

And he drew out his soul with great strength.

Sertorius, who was caught accidentally,

Fell on the sword when the enemy entered:

"Where is the commander?" he asked.

"There is no commander!" he replied.

Thus he equaled himself to his ancestor.

No, more! He surpassed him.

The destruction of Carthage - a rare feat...

But the greater achievement - overcoming death!

Believe me, Seneca, death will not be terrifying

For those who do not value life.

Receive it as the last jest of fate,

Watching with calmness the anger of your enemies.

May you be pure in conscience without sin,

There are some judges who are cowards...

Trust in a just decision,

But beware, for to the worst, Seneca, be prepared.

Who distinguished turmoil from its cause?

Only fear is terrifying.

Men, like children, wear a mask of courage

Why frighten us with sharp edges?

Why lure people into danger with jests?

Not less painful is the birth of a stone in the kidneys,

And for women - the child they carry.

I shall fasten my belt and be among many,

Exiled - I will regain my homeland,

My legs are bound by thorns,

I shall die - and forget this turmoil.

We die quietly, day by day,

Like drops that flow out of a clock.

Yesterday's day has perished - undoubtedly,

For time cannot be sat down on.

Only humans can be foolishly afraid

Of death in fear of it.

The wise man does not flee from life loudly,

He withdraws like water between the rocks.

Some people are consumed by life

and cannot overcome their aversion to it.

They rush to gather their family at the last hour.

Do not hurry.

Be healthy.
