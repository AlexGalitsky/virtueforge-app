### Philosophical Context
Letter XXIV by Seneca is a response to a letter from Lucilius, who expresses concern about courage and the ability to face fear and hardship. Seneca offers his thoughts on how to live with difficulties and achieve tranquility.

### Essence
- Seneca asserts that courage is not about avoiding difficulties but rather about confronting them with dignity.
- He cites historical figures such as Rufus and Socrates, who exemplified courage in the face of death and other hardships.
- Seneca also emphasizes the importance of conscience and moral principle in decision-making.

### Connection to Virtue
The virtue of courage is linked to integrity, a key aspect of virtus in Stoic philosophy. Seneca argues that a person who can face fear and hardship with dignity demonstrates their virtue.

### Question to Ponder
Who among people will be capable of facing fear and hardship with dignity?
