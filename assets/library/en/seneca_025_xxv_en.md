**Letter XXV (On Correcting Faults)**

Dear Seneca,

There are two paths here:

In one, we must correct our flaws,
In the other, we must eradicate our vices.

You will say that some cannot be changed:

After forty years...no doctor can find a cure for the soul.

We should not set limits for ourselves,
For chronic illnesses require treatment.

I prefer to remain unsuccessful,
Than to bury my faith without trying.

A virtuous thought in the heart produces an echo,
Which may change the soul.

The second will blush with shame,
And we must support this shame,
For only shame can prevent

It from committing its next sin.

With a veteran, one must be cautious,
Lest he regain his confidence.

Perhaps his vices have retreated?

They will return soon, troubling my soul.

You are right that limiting our load
Of desires and obligations is a burden.

These petty pleasures carry us on the path to bliss without restraint.

"Everything should be done under the supervision of Epicurus."

This is my gift. Let us curb vice

Together with philosophy and culture,
Planting sin in an unshakeable fortress.

Until you become a good person,
Lest you commit sin in the shade of dense woods,

Let there always be someone nearby,
Watching over you.

Be healthy.
