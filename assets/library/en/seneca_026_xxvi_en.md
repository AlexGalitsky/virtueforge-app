**Letter XXVI (On the Readiness for Death)**

Dear Seneca,

I feel that my old age is behind me,
That weakness is a burden I can barely lift.
But my heart is full of joy.

My sins are destroyed with my body,
My soul is experiencing its prime.

I laugh and say, "I have no business
With many who are weary from life's toll."

I must calm myself down,
And figure out what modesty demands now:

Is it wisdom that calls me to live with dignity?
Or is it age that tells me the door has closed?

I don't want to do anything beyond my limits...

But I believe this, and I'm glad.

For though my body has worn out,
Why should I strive for more?

This is true happiness:
Not falling, but gently disappearing,
Nature softens our fate,
And daily light loses its intensity.

Death will show me what achievements
I've prepared for my final day without pride.

Is there a reason to be proud of myself?
Is it not just deception and pretence when I speak?

Throw away the opinions of others - they're unreliable...

Throw away the knowledge that has led us astray...

Whatever anyone says, it's all insignificant,
When death pronounces its verdict.

Here is my gift: "Think about death!"

(As Epictetus once said, "Why think about death? What should trouble you
For the days of joy that are allotted to you?")
What can we test by experience?
And what can our minds comprehend?

Whoever is not afraid of death is free.
Freedom is the greatest gift!
