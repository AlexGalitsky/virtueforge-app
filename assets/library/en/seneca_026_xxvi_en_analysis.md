### Philosophical Context
This text is a philosophical letter from the Senecan period, in which the author speaks to Luiciana about the readiness for death. It reflects Seneca's philosophical thought that freedom and happiness can only be found through the acceptance of one's mortality.

### Essence
* Seneca emphasizes the importance of readiness for death as the key to freedom and happiness.
* He asserts that vices perish with the body, while the soul flourishes, implying that we should focus on the development of moral virtue.
* Seneca also underscores the importance of disregarding societal opinions and scientific views that may limit us, and instead concentrating on personal experience and happiness.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca connects readiness for death with the development of moral virtue, arguing that only then can we find freedom and happiness. This aligns with the idea that virtue is the key to happiness, as discussed in Franklin's Week Order.
