# The Letter XXVII (On Borrowed Virtue)

Dear Seneca,

Do you, who are suffering, write to me?

I am writing to myself in my journal,
And I do not consider this bad.

I scold myself on paper,
Screaming: "You, like a child!"
Believe that, from shame, I
Am unable to look into the mirror.

Why should I worry about pleasures,
When they cost so dearly?

The vices are thieves who haunt me,
And troubles pierce my bones.

It is only virtue that is not empty,
For it holds the promise of joy.

Just like clouds, blown by the wind,
Cannot last forever and hide the light.

When will we attain this joy?
How many of us can rush to meet it?

Hard work invested, you will receive a reward,
Know that this labor cannot be transferred.

I remember Calvinyus Sabine,
A man who was incredibly wealthy in two words,

But his memory was so weak that he
Could not even mention his own name.

He wanted to be a cultured person,
Ordered eleven slaves to buy the names of all the poets forever

So their names would fly away from his teeth.

He began reading poetry at the table with guests,

As much as he could.

But he always got stuck, like bones,

And choked on a lump.

Satellius, smiling in secret,
Advised him to take up wrestling:

"You're alive only because you didn't fight!
Why not yourself?"

Take more slaves.
The soul cannot be bought or borrowed from someone else,
There would be some who would want it:
No one asks for greatness,
But everyone wants lowliness.

"Riches are poverty by nature"

Says Epikuros under his own roof.

How can I spread this idea to the people?

Be healthy.

—
