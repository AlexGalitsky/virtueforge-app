# The Futility of Travel

Dear Seneca,

You are surprised that changing one's location and embarking on journeys did not help you escape the torment that consumes your soul.

As Socrates said, "It is indeed strange that you toil at sea, lamenting."

There will be no benefit from distant travels when you carry your own inner sorrow with you wherever you go.

What holds us back in our wanderings? It is ourselves, weighed down by our internal anguish.

Until we shed the burden of our soul's grief, it will not find peace anywhere.

You long to cast off this weight, but the load on the ship holds you back. Give it time, and the vessel will either sink or remain afloat.

A calm mind is at ease wherever it is, just as if it were at home.

Even if we are cast out into the farthest reaches of the earth, all people are the same to me, with whom I am familiar through my words alone.

Traveling does not add strength to us, but rather a beard.

Who wanders? Those who roam, my dear...

But to live in accordance with truth is given to us everywhere.

Who loves to live in turmoil and worry? Who finds joy in new troubles?

That person is foolish. A wise man prefers peace above all else.

What good is it to rid oneself of vices for the sake of fighting against others? Avoid places where people quarrel, forgetting their deadlines.

Socrates fought against the tyranny of tyrants, valuing freedom of speech above all else.

He who has killed his own slave within himself will never change in any way.

"Understanding the problem is the first step to healing."

Here is Epictetus' wisdom, which surpasses that of doctors:

Accept it with your soul,

And

Be healthy.

—
