### Philosophical Context
Letter XXVIII by Seneca is a response to Lucretia, who expresses her frustration that travel and change of scenery have not alleviated her melancholy and restlessness. This text reflects the philosophical idea that true freedom and happiness can only be achieved through internal transformation and the awareness of one's emotions and thoughts.

### Core Message
* Travel and change of scenery cannot alleviate melancholy and restlessness if one carries internal burdens.
* Internal melancholy is the primary obstacle for individuals.
* Wisdom lies in recognizing a flaw and taking steps to rectify it.
* Living in truth and avoiding places where people argue and forget about deadlines.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Travel and change of scenery cannot alleviate melancholy and restlessness if one carries internal burdens that need to be addressed.

### Connection to Virtue
In this text, the importance of internal transformation and the awareness of one's emotions and thoughts as the foundation for achieving virtue is emphasized. This aligns with the idea that wisdom lies in recognizing a flaw and rectifying it.

### Question to Ponder
Who can recognize and address their internal burdens to achieve true freedom and happiness?
