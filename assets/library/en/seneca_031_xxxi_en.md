# The Labor of Life

You, Socrates, are gaining a noble spirit and stature.

Place remarkable milestones in your path,
Dare to touch them with your hand.

To become wise, close your ears tighter,
More securely than the wax of the Sirens:
Ulysses passed by the whirlpool, unable to listen,
And you - the whole world can plunge into chaos.

For even those who value you most,
Do not see evil in your good intentions.

Trust yourself. Let the wind study your skin,
Let your hair be tangled by the storms...

It is not ease that is good, but rather the struggle.
I am among those who are admired for
Enduring hunger and cold and nakedness

In a single burst of courage, they overcome their inclination.

Labor and virtue are the sources of happiness.
And in darkness - the root of evil.

What do we pray for? Is it not knowledge?
And malice? It is ignorance and human foolishness.

Discipline yourself, with an unwavering hand,
Seek labor, not to be deterred by its seeming insignificance.

Do not let the thought that your labor is insignificant
Distract you. Let it seem as though it is only a shadow of effort:
Your labor will help your soul grow.

Do not falter, for this is what men are accustomed to,
But to strive for perfection in yourself,
You must exclude any flaw or distinction.

Then you will attain bliss with God,
And be near Him, not apart from Him.

God is modest, but He is the one who sums up all things,
Only through Him do we fall and rise again.
We call a high spirit "God".
Only ornaments - gold and silver.

Look at yourself critically and strictly:
Are you worthy of God?

Be healthy.
