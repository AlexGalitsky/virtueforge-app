### Philosophical Context
This text is part of a philosophical work by Seneca, in which he emphasizes the importance of labor and virtue in achieving happiness and well-being. The phrase "Labor, virtue - in them lies the source of happiness" reflects Stoicism, a philosophical school that underscores the importance of rational deliberation and decision-making rather than emotional reactions.

### Core Message
* Labor and virtue are the primary sources of happiness.
* One must be prepared for labor and not be deterred by its insignificance.
* The soul must exclude all discord and strife to achieve perfection.
* We call the high spirit God, and adornments gold and silver.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Labor and virtue are the primary sources of happiness, which can only be attained through prolonged and arduous effort.

### Connection to Virtue
In this text, the importance of virtue as the key to happiness is highlighted. Seneca stresses that virtue is the primary source of happiness, not prosperity or material possessions.

### Question to Contemplate
How can I exclude all discord and strife from myself to achieve perfection?
