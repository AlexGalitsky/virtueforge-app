### Philosophical Context
The text "Letter XXXII" was written by Seneca, a Roman philosopher and politician, in which he shared his thoughts on self-confidence and the importance of striving for freedom. This text is part of his work "Epistulae Morales ad Lucilium," where he develops the idea that a person should be free from external influences and personal biases.

### Essence
* The text emphasizes the importance of escaping external influences and striving for freedom.
* Seneca underscores the necessity of being true to oneself, rather than following others or imitating virtue.
* He also highlights the importance of preserving freedom in time and destiny.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Believing in oneself is a key concept related to virtue, as it presupposes an awareness of one's own strengths and limitations, as well as a readiness to act according to one's own principles. This requires a person to be free from external influences and biases, which is a crucial aspect of virtue.
