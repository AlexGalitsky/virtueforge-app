### Philosophical Context
This text is a letter from Lucretia to Seneca, emphasizing the importance of frequency and efficacy in philosophical discourse and raising the question of how wisdom should be expressed.

### Essence
- The value of letters lies not in their profundity but in their frequency and continuity.
- Philosophy is counsel, not grandiloquent words.
- To wise individuals, the essence of speech is noble, whereas loud cries belong to the nature of beasts.
- A word of instruction can take root in a person's soul if it is expressed in accordance with individual needs and desires.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Wisdom and the noble essence of speech are paramount, while ungrammatical cries are out of place in a society where honesty and justice should prevail.

### Question to Ponder
What is the difference between judgment and counsel?
