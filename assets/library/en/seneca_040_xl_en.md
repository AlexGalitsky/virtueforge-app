# The Speed of Speech

Lucilius greets Seneca.

Portraits are a means to alleviate melancholy.

Though we remain distant, the joy of familiar hands is found in letters.

You write about what I heard from Serapion,
whose speech was like a waterfall, unlabored and effortless.

A philosopher speaks concisely and persuasively,
while those who speak at length often stumble over their words.

The forceful delivery and verbosity
are suited to orators speaking before crowds.

Those who are worthy of the company of philosophers
should be content to read slowly and carefully.

In speeches, where people grasp the truth,
there should be simplicity and clarity.

However, those who do not understand the speeches
need excitement and speed.

Such speeches have noise, but no substance,
and noise... (remember the fable about dogs)

The wise will rise from their graves
unscathed by the tumult of words.

One who is swift in speech without soul
is like a person who rushes to a bend without being able to turn back.

He who is wise does not hurry, remains calm

There is no need to rush to instruct others.

"Can I not raise my voice?" 

No, you can, but dignity must be preserved.

The Lord has counted every hair,
how can we change with words?

Vinius always spoke in a "stretching" style...

"What do you say?" - exclaimed the fool:

For foolishness is heavy on the mind,
like a heavy load of walking sticks.

Let them bellow in Greece, trying to hurry,
but it's more natural for us to divide our thoughts.

Thus Cicero, as if looking back,
could heal with words.

And Fabius, who lived without flaw,
was wise and eloquent (although he might be rebuked).

He could convey his meaning within five minutes
without the need for rapid speech.

Whoever speaks faster than they understand
has no conscience, no shame,

and forgets that others are taking in what they say,
and will find trouble in their own words.

Return to your diligence with things,
and then speak - they are but a trifle.

Whoever is familiar with the truth speaks clearly and carefully

And weighs his words.
