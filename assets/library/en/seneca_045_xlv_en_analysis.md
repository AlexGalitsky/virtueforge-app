### Philosophical Context
This text is part of Seneca's philosophical letters, in which he explores various aspects of life and morality. In this letter, he discusses the concept of truth and its likeness, emphasizing the importance of seeking truth and distinguishing between verbal expressions and actual truth.

### Essence
- Truth is not always apparent and can be concealed.
- Desires and intentions may be at odds.
- Happiness does not necessarily imply the absence of reasoning but may involve the pursuit of true understanding.
- Life passes more quickly than we can contemplate, and we must live rather than plan.

### Connection to Virtue
Correspondingly, the concept of truth and its likeness relates to the idea of honesty. Honesty involves seeking truth and being transparent and truthful in one's actions and words.

### Question to Ponder
Who can be the truth if everything in life is a lie?
