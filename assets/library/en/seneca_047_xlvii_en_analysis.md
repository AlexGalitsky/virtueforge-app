### Philosophical Context
Seneca's letter to Lucilius is a response to her inquiry about slaves, in which she expresses concern over the treatment of slaves and emphasizes the importance of moral principles. In this letter, Seneca analyzes the concept of slavery and underscores the necessity of considering the moral consequences of how we treat others.

### Essence
- Seneca asserts that all people are equal and should be treated with dignity, regardless of their social status.
- He emphasizes the importance of moral principles and ethics in our dealings with others.
- Seneca also criticizes the treatment of slaves as inappropriate and unjust.
- He argues that all people are "fellow slaves" and should be treated with respect.

### Form of This Version
Seneca's letter is written in prose, where he analyzes the concept of slavery and stresses the necessity of moral principles in our interactions with others.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca connects the treatment of people to the concepts of morality and ethical principles. He argues that everyone should be treated with dignity and respect, irrespective of their social standing.
