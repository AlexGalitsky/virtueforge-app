### Philosophical Context
This text is a letter from Seneca to Lucilius, in which he critiques the philosophy of the Sophists. Sophisms are complex and contradictory arguments used to justify various moral and ethical positions.

### Essence
- Seneca asserts that sophisms are meaningless and cannot lead to truth.
- He criticizes the way Sophists use language and logic to create contradictions and justifications that are not grounded in real truth.
- Seneca emphasizes the importance of seeking truth and genuine understanding in philosophy.

### Form of This Version
Prose

### Connection to Virtue
By linking sophisms to egoistic thoughts, Seneca underscores the importance of the interconnectedness and divisibility of virtues. In this sense, his ideas about truth and honesty are tied to the concept of virtue as a foundational moral theory.
