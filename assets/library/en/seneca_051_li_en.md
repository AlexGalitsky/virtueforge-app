# The Art of Dwelling
---

Dear Seneca,

What is the ideal place to reside? Are the peaks, plains, steppes, or rivers the answer?

Each location has its unique charm.

I once stayed in the resort town of Baiae, but I left on the very same day. I prefer to spend my nights in a forest hut rather than indulging in the excesses of the resort.

"Should we forget the places where we have been before?" is not a straightforward question, dear friend...

As with wise men, one does not wear flashy clothes; similarly, one should not indulge in loud revelry. The Canopium is a den of vices and pleasures that are unaware of conscience. It's difficult to escape the pitfalls of deception there.

Just as you need to take care of your health, so too do you need to tend to your character. Let us choose suitable places where one does not see drunkards or those without shame or conscience.

There, the inhabitants of the ghetérê (a type of brothel) reign supreme, having forgotten the strict Shepherd. The allure of sin is strong, and they will only be tamed with a deadline...

Even Gnaeus Hannibal, the mighty Carthaginian, was not immune to this temptation.

Campania! A place of great charm and beauty...

But the winter months corrupted all who stayed there. Rome rose again when it was not the lions that were devouring the sheep.

We are soldiers, but our duty does not allow us to take a break. We must conquer pleasure as well as defeat.

I do not need hot springs or a cool tavern to refresh me...

The most useful thing is to sweat through hard work until one's strength wanes.

We will not follow Hannibal's path of indulgence, lest we be rebuked for our laziness.

In the flags of Punic glory, there is much pride... But we have a chance at fortune ahead.

If we do not succumb to pleasure, then our virtues and anger will be our allies. Poverty and suffering are the enemies that will destroy us if we let them.

I shall try! I have been offered freedom...

Not to be bound by the fate of earthly destiny.

For when one's fate sees no escape from death, it will turn away helplessly from struggle.

Leaving behind the plow, the sound of war drums fills my soul with passion... But once worn out, one loses strength and is consumed by dust in battle.

Gaius Marius, Gnaeus Pompeius, and Caesar, all built their greatness on the hills of Baiae.

It is fitting for a warrior to do so...

The fear that lingers from fortresses in valleys is not to be underestimated.

Why should Catiline look into the window at a drunken revelry?

Born not as a gull but as a man,

I will forget the sounds of the flute, but not the trumpet.

Let us run, pursue, and tear ourselves away with our hearts...

Conquer sin, even if it's just for a moment... For in sin is a wound in the heart, and that wound is the pleasure.

Be healthy.
