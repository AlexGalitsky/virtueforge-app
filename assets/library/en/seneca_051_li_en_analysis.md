### Philosophical Context
In this letter, Seneca addresses the question of where to choose to live, emphasizing the importance of avoiding places where one might fall into vices and pleasures. This is tied to Seneca's belief that those living in pastoral simplicity cannot indulge in external pleasures.

### Essence
- Vices and pleasures can be powerful and unpredictable.
- Places with a large number of people and entertainments can become breeding grounds for vices and pleasures.
- We must avoid these places to prevent ourselves from succumbing to them.
- Through strength and effort, we can conquer vices and pleasures rather than allowing them to conquer us.

### Connection to Virtue
This aligns with the principle of honesty and justice, which underscores the importance of avoiding vices and pleasures. It also relates to the idea that those living in pastoral simplicity should be stricter with themselves and not allow vices and pleasures to influence their lives.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us can conquer vices and pleasures without the help of others?
