# The Letter LIII (On the Diseases of Body and Soul)

Lucillia greets Seneca.

I set out under the open sky, full of clouds,
And soon "earned my pay" with nuts,
When a gust of wind from the mountains arrived.

The whole sea, which at first was calm,
Was covered by a chilling wave,
In my stomach it became unpleasant,
Even though they told me: "The storm is not to be feared..."

I approached the helm, wanting to rule the shore,
But he just laughed at me in response:

It's better to sail in open seas in a storm,
There are no anchorages nearby.

Then, when I saw the cliffs, overcome with grief,
I immediately jumped overboard

(Ulysses was doomed by the wrath of the sea
He didn't drown, but he couldn't tolerate the rocking).

I immediately remembered the art of sailing,
Then a greasy rock came into view,
Then, when my stomach was empty,
I lay trembling like sand,

And thought: how easily I can forget my ailments

Physical ones that signal to us...

Spiritual ones: only briefly

They are stronger than our fear.

When the fever comes to visit me,

At first, it manifests with a slight heat,
Which rises secretly,
It strikes with a crushing blow...

The diseases of the soul are the opposite:

Who is affected by them does not want to listen
To doctors, who just tell him: "Close your mouth..."

Who sleeps poorly remembers his dreams,
Even if they are unclear.

But if a deep sleep fills my spirit

Then, over my soul, we have more freedom.

Tell me: who admits their flaws?

Who can condemn himself in them?

We sleep... And who wakes us up at the right time?

The philosopher is the one who can wake us up.

Who falls ill, he sets aside all his worries,
 Cancels appointments, drinks a powder,
Forgets about the undone work...

The disease of the soul is far more terrifying than shock.

Give back to philosophy all the time:

You don't dedicate just an hour to it,

It commands: "Push your foot forward"

And stronger with the other leg.

Thus said Alexander the Great,

On his offer of tribute: "I'm not joking,

I won't take from you what you have to give,

I'll leave for you whatever I want."

Even the gods, who live a long life,
All their deeds are like miracles.

But, in one thing... the wise man is above God,

Delivering himself from fear.

There is no bravery greater than that of the philosopher...

The spear flies with a light swing of the veer

It returns to him who throws it,

And... it strikes back.

Be healthy.
