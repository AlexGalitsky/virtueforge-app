# Letter LIV (On Illness)

Dear Seneca,

I have been beset by an old illness, which is taking its toll on me.

How so? - I am short of breath, as if I were wading through snow for hours, perhaps an hour or two, like climbing a mountain.

I am familiar with any disease, but this one is the most severe of all:

I foresee that my spirit will soon be exhausted.

Death is far more terrifying than illness.

You think I am cheerful because I have survived?

The delay is not victory, dear friend...

For death narrows down to a circle: it draws closer and closer...

And finally, the last circle awaits me.

Why do you keep coming back to visit me for so long?

I know death: it is non-being...

As with birth - I could not laugh before...

Just as after death - what is there left for me?

The candle has either burned out or gone out...

It matters little... As long as the flame burns, it lives and shines.

We should all wish that! Without light, only darkness remains...

I have said this, even when I thought: The door is locked...

(But my words were not spoken aloud...)

Until my cough subsided,

I confess - it is hard to breathe.

My breathing is working poorly...

Nature's law is to breathe!

Let the air stagnate... What's the difference?

It is frightening to gasp from the depths of one's soul...

Death will not bar its door for a wise man

He will leave on his own when the time comes,

Aware that he must go, and believing in fate.

Be healthy.
