### Philosophical Context
The epistolary poem by Seneca to Lucilius expresses his concern over her health and Death as an inevitable reality. This text reflects philosophical musings on the nature of death, virtue, and the significance of life.

### Essence
* Seneca conveys his worry about Lucilius' illness and Death as an inescapable reality.
* He emphasizes that Death is not something to be feared but rather a natural consequence of life.
* Seneca asserts that death is a state of non-being, one we are familiar with from birth but cannot mock after death.
* He also underscores the importance of life and how it shines in the world.

### Form of This Version
prose

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca ties his thoughts on Death to the concept of virtue. He argues that a wise person will not flee from death but will depart voluntarily when the time comes. This underscores the importance of virtue in life and the ability to deal with death.

### Question to Ponder
Why is Death not something to be feared by a wise person?
