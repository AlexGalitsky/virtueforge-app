# The Nature of Things

Lucilius greets Seneca!

Let us discuss the scarcity of language and its subjects in the library.

Take, for example, detestable insects that pursue our livestock:

"Oistros" or "ovod" are familiar to us?

In Virgil's works, who can find this word?

Again, Virgil says "let us settle the dispute with iron."

It has been known since ancient times...

A simple word becomes meaningless.

We say "we resolve the dispute,"

My citations lack reverence,

I do not think or pour out wisdom.

The Aetolian League and Ennia are forgotten,

And "The Aeneid" is no longer remembered.

You ask: What is the purpose of a preface?

Where does my judgment lead you?

I want you to hear this word: Being...

I risk eternal fame, courtesy of Cicero...

New? I entrust it to Fabian...

It is the foundation of every law,

A gift from nature that grants reason.

There is a word that is not heard in Latin:

"To he" - a simple word with only a sound.

We replace it with a synonym now,

"Synonym" "what exists" - by meaning, a poor word...

Plato distinguished things in six aspects,

I will list them... before explaining:

Is there - a kind? and in thoughts?

Following the original idea,

They are united by a single thread.

What is man? It is known - he is a kind.

And horse, dog - also kinds...

But the common root? - In "animal" it has developed.

There is a second aspect - what is elevated above others:

"The poet" - not just someone who knows the meter...

Only Greeks considered him one - Homer.

Is there a third kind - everything that exists,

But within our limits?

(Ideas, as Plato calls them),

There is a standard for imitation, but...

I will explain, adding my interpretation:

Imagine I was writing your portrait,

I would have expressed the charm of the face...

(idea), preserving it for centuries.

The fourth aspect - elsoe...this is subtlety

(Vindicting Plato, I do not harbor resentment...)

The example of art is clear even to children:

Model - idea, elsoe - the portrait itself.

The fifth kind is very simple to resolve:

Objects and animals - everything is in it.

The sixth includes things that relate to us:

Space, time, where we live.

Everything we see or touch,

Plato did not consider them among his numbers:

They come and go, never remaining the same.

"We enter, but cannot re-enter the same stream."

Death comes to each of us one day,

Like our final life's conclusion.

You ask: What is the benefit of these classes?

The poet has looked out of the window,

And suddenly saw...the wing of Pegasus...

We are not allowed to create without rest,

I want to turn any knowledge into a benefit:

What can Plato give me?

In his "ideas" - an excuse for recognition:

I see in the surrounding world "nothing".

Everything exists only in imagination,

A meaningless word, like the sound.

The path of the spirit - in true movement

To the immortal, as commanded by God.

Nothing is eternal, but Being alone is eternal.

Who does not understand - will be trapped in the flesh's power.

Therefore - whether you want it or not:

The material lives in His hands.

And perhaps our fate may be extended for a little longer,

For those who could (the task is quite complex),

Subdue and control vice.

Plato, born "broad-shouldered",

Cared for everything with moderation:

By abstinence alone, more than anything else,

He lived to the age of nine.

He died on the same day he was born,

Living nine times nine years.

The Persian priests honored him as a sacrifice,

For those who saw in him...the lotus.

When old age turns into decrepitude,

It is easy to end it...

Only a drunkard can empty a bucket with wine,

Pouring bitterness of decay.

But if the body cannot endure service,

Is it not better to give freedom to the soul?

And, doing this a little earlier than necessary,

Although, of course, we should not hurry.

I ask you not to listen against your will,

But, on my leisure time, weigh my words:

When the body has worn out its willpower,

Its spirit has gained the right to leave.

I will not flee from death due to illness,

That heals without touching the soul...

But, in the face of constant pain,

It is better to destroy life.

Note: The original text is a philosophical letter written by Lucilius to Seneca, discussing various aspects of language and reality.
