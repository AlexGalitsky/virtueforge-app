### Philosophical Context
Letter LXXX is a philosophical letter by Seneca, in which he discusses the nature of language and its connections to Platonic ideas. In this letter, Seneca analyzes the differences between genera and species, as well as their relationship to the concept of "being."

### Core
- Genera and species represent two distinct levels of understanding reality.
- A genus is a general primary type of something that serves as the foundation for understanding a species.
- A species is a specific type or class of something that differs from other species.
- Plato distinguished six genera: existence, form, concept, poet, idea, and immortality.
- Seneca examines these genera and their relationship to the concept of "being."
- He asserts that being is a general genus that encompasses all other genera.
