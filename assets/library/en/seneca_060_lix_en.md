# The Joy and the Lie

Lucilia salutes Seneca with joy.

I found no fault in his words,

Only the allure of wisdom itself,

A faith only in one's own good fortune.

And strength, in him - a lifted spirit...

He is not stingy with words, like an old miser,

But the sense, faster than words, rushes by.

In life, some find reward in position,

Others in marriage, or a flock of doves,

The birth of a son... Is this joy?

Or is it often accompanied by sorrow?

There is one sign that is always present:

Joy cannot be turned into a source of woe.

It is not to be confused with pleasure - that's an illusion.

Moments of happiness, given by the wind...

Sometimes, there are fools and deceivers,

Who are happy for the right reasons,

But their blessings are unknown to them,

When their spirit is lifted and aglow.

Your letter was concise, well-aimed,

No unnecessary words, no excess.

No echo of words that could be trimmed,

Leaving only a clear and direct thought.

It has poetry, it has vivid metaphors...

Let the critics criticize us for this

I am ready to call upon the ancient wise men.

Words are not used for decoration,

How can they be used by a poet?

Their accuracy is guarded from mistakes,

By those who leave their mark.

Quintus Sextius Niger wrote: "The army marches in formation,

Waiting for the unseen battle...

And the wise build their virtues like this:

So that they can hear each other's signals."

For foolishness is all fear, and no peace,

It haunts itself with its own shadow.

A wise man, on the other hand, is not disturbed,

By his own shame, poverty, pain, or laziness.

We have long been infected by our vices,

And it's hard to clean ourselves:

We give a weak response to foolishness,

And we don't see depths in wisdom.

Our greatest enemy is self-love,

We love flattery and put faces on it,

And we follow those who echo it, like an echo...

We know that everyone has their own faith,

When they think they understand everything,

The murderer is celebrated for his humility of hand,

And the prostitute is placed on the throne,

And people say: "You are selfless."

But, remember, even the beasts know moderation,

In food and drink... They eat only what they can.

Who is not sad and arrogant?

Who does not look to tomorrow with a tremble in their heart?

The one who has a calm spirit, strict and unchanging,

Has achieved everything that a human can achieve.

Who seeks wealth, honor, and glory,

Is far from the wise, happy people.

Do not float on the surface of joy,

Only with wisdom do you stay steady.

Who seeks happiness in luxury, parties,

Will never find it: a broken toy...

Drinking, revelry... These are but fleeting pleasures,

While true joy burns like a fire that never goes out.

The applause, the cheers, the shouts of admiration:

"He is Master! He is Lord! He is God!"

Pass away, and come back with revenge,

And payback for lies.

"What, can't even be happy? No?"

Yes, they can, like the cunning lions,

Who feast on pleasure "rewards".

Pain appears, and woe...

The one who indulges in pleasures every night

Does not see the burning fires,

While true joy is eternal and unchanging,

And never fades away.

Be healthy.
