### Philosophical Context
In this letter, Lucrèce Déker critiques the notion that joy is the primary virtue of a person. She emphasizes the importance of distinguishing between joy and pleasure, and raises questions about how we can truly be happy.

### Essence
- Joy does not become a source of misery.
- Joy is a moment of happiness bestowed by the muse.
- Critique of flattery and the pursuit of wisdom.
- Wisdom is the ability to see the depth of things.
- Folly is the fear that frightens its own shadow.
- The wise are protected from folly.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Lucrèce Déker's letter critically assesses the idea that joy is the primary virtue of a person.

### Connection to Virtue
Virtue is a key aspect of wisdom and joy. In this letter, Lucrèce underscores the importance of seeking wisdom and critically evaluating flattery to achieve true happiness.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us can truly be joyful rather than merely trying to satisfy our own desires?
