### Philosophical Context
Letter LX from Seneca is a response to Lucretia's complaints about her quarrels and anger, in which she expresses her uncertainty and anxiety that her greed exceeds natural reason.

### Essence
* Lucretia emphasizes that her desires surpass her capacity for satisfaction.
* She compares her greed to the needs of other animals and sea creatures, which cannot store food within themselves.
* She also highlights that her avarice is a problem that could lead to negative consequences.

### Connection to Virtue
Lucretia's greed exemplifies how human nature can be contradictory. This underscores the importance of considering the moral consequences of our actions and striving for a simpler and more just way of life.
