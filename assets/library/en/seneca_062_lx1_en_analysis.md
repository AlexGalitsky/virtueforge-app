### Philosophical Context
Letter LXI is part of a sequence of essays by Seneca, in which he explores the concept of virtue and the attitude towards death. This letter is a personal missive in which Seneca expresses his readiness to embrace death as a means to escape the harmful aspects of life.

### Core
- Seneca asserts that he cannot deny his past wrongdoings but aims to focus on positivity and the joys of life.
- He wishes to die willingly, not against his will, and believes this is possible for those who are bound by the moral knot of virtue.
- Seneca argues that misfortune lies not in being a slave but in having one's thoughts centered on vice and objections.
- He contends that one who is bound by the moral knot of virtue can remember the finiteness of life without sorrow.

### Form of This Version
A written form utilizing metaphor (a tight sea knot) and personal expressions.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca links the readiness to embrace death to being bound by the moral knot of virtue, asserting that one who is so bound can remember the finiteness of life without sorrow.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us is truly bound by the moral knot of virtue and can remember the finiteness of life with eagerness?
