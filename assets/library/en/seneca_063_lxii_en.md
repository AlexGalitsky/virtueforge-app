# Letter LXII (On Occupations)

Dear Seneca,

The one who is overly occupied is, in truth, deceiving himself by becoming entangled in an endless pursuit. He, as a result, becomes weak and pale.

I, on the other hand, yield to my tasks only when necessary, but I do not seek opportunities to indulge in them.

By saving myself from idle pursuits, I avoid burdening my soul with empty endeavors. I will not let my soul be troubled by vain thoughts.

With friends, I am never alone, for I remain with those who are dear to me. Though I may spend time with the better ones, I find myself drawn into unknown paths.

Indeed, "our fellowship is truly beautiful!"

Demetrius, your purple robes do not dazzle me as much as our conversations do.

Even wealth can be mocked and ridiculed. You have surrendered to the struggle, while others still cling to it.

Wisdom begins in the depths of one's soul, where greed has been subdued. Do not listen to the voice of greed, which cries out from within your heart.

Be healthy.

—
