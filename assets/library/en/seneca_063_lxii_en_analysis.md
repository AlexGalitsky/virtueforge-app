### Philosophical Context
Letter LXII by Seneca is a response to a letter from Demetrius, in which Demetrius asserts that wealth is the primary virtue of a person. Seneca, however, emphasizes the importance of freedom and balance in life.

### Essence
- Seneca argues that being busy does not necessarily equate to productivity or the creation of value.
- He states that "he who is much occupied is more likely to be lying to himself," meaning that a person who is constantly engaged in activities may feel exhausted and drained.
- Seneca underscores the importance of maintaining freedom and balance in life rather than striving for wealth or material happiness.
- He also asserts that "saving one's soul, actions are a vain burden" and that one should prioritize their inner well-being.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca ties his thoughts on the importance of freedom and balance in life to the concept of virtue (in virtus). He argues that a person who prioritizes their inner well-being and freedom is a more virtuous individual.
