### Philosophical Context
Letter LXIII by Seneca is a response to a letter from Lucretia, who expresses sorrow over the death of her friend Flaccus. In this letter, Seneca offers a philosophical perspective on death and mourning.

### Essence
- Seneca asserts that weeping is not an appropriate response to the loss of a loved one.
- He argues that the memory of the deceased can be pleasant, but this does not mean that we should weep.
- Seneca also emphasizes the importance of making decisions and taking action in one's life rather than merely indulging in grief.
