# Letter LXV (On Common Causes)

Dear Seneca,

I have spent the day beset by troubles:

From dawn till dusk, I was plagued by distractions,
so that I could not even sit in my chair.

Then I wrote, and my friends arrived,
and I spent the rest of the day engaged in conversation.

I will recount what we did not discuss.

Who should one support: their friends or oneself?

The cause and matter are at stake

In nature (the Stoics' motto).

The matter is dull without reason,
but reason turns it upside down.

Art is in imitation of nature.

Look at the statue: the material is visible
(like bronze), but the artist has given it form, like a rose.

A Stoic believes there is one cause
of what things can create.

But Aristotle divided them into four:
the "what" (material), the "who" (creator), the "form" (name), and the "intention".

I will explain: first, the material itself,
second, who created it,
third, its form (and the name given to it),
and fourth, its intention.

Let us consider them one by one:

First, the material itself is bronze,
second, the artist (he is the creator),
and third, its form (the rose).

We will consider intention last:
why did the artist take up this work?

Was it for hire? Then follow the money,
or was it to gain fame and reputation?

Or perhaps he believed in a higher power?
Did he think of going to a temple?

Plato added a fifth cause:

"The idea" or, more simply, the model.

By representing truth in his mind,
the artist raises his instrument.

So, we have five causes according to Plato:
the "what", the "who", the external form,
the similarity (hold back your sighs),
and the "for what"...

All of these are present in the universe:

The material is the foundation,
God is the creator,
and the form is the eternal order.

What does God intend?

One: "Whoever is good, creates only good".

Although some people may be wicked,
they need bread, wine, and silver.

Plato and Aristotle gave us too many causes,
while others gave us too few...

Time and space are needed to create something new.

But we seek the common cause,

which is simple as material itself.

It is clear: God's essence is present
in all things, from human to Christ.

What is God? - I will answer immediately

(Perhaps scribes will portray me as a fool):

He is the All-Beneficent and Active Reason,
that cannot be contained in any image.

You say that cause is form,
intention, or model?

There are no causes, only instruments with norms:

like a brush, pen, saw, and chisel.

How would you solve this complex problem?

Or will you leave it unsolved?

What about the universe? - Is death
the end, or is it a new existence?

I do not fear any representation:

The soul will not be confined.

Be healthy.
