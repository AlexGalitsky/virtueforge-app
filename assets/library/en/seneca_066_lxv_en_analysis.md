### Philosophical Context
Letter LXV is a spontaneous letter from Seneca to Lucilius, in which Seneca discusses the nature of causality and its connection to virtue.

### Essence
- Plato provided five causes: material, efficient, formal, exemplary, and final.
- The Stoics believe there is one cause for why things are created.
- Aristotle divided causes into four categories: material, efficient, formal, and final.
- Seneca emphasizes the importance of understanding causes for achieving virtue.

### Form of This Version
prose

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca connects the concept of causality to virtue, asserting that understanding causes can help individuals achieve virtue. This aligns with the idea that virtue is the result of the right choices and actions.
