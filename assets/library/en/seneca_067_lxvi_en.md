# The Nature of the Good

Lucilla salutes Seneca.

I met Clarus, a fellow student,
who, although old, has a strong spirit,
and though his body is frail, his soul remains unbroken.
Perhaps nature wanted to show us that
even in the most refined bodies,
the good can reside.

The soul is our true beauty, not the body;
for it is not the body that requires adornment.
A brave warrior, who charges into battle with courage,
is more beautiful than any satyr.

We have been discussing the nature of the good...

Are they all equal? Is there a higher good?
In worldly affairs, should we seek something like this?
What can be taken to heart?

First and foremost, let us strive for prosperity
for our country, health, joy, and peace on earth.
Secondly, in the trials and hardships of life,
let us learn to endure and persevere.
Thirdly, let us cultivate a dignified demeanor,
a calm and open face, a gentle gait,
good food, and a comfortable home.

The first thing is to have a strong spirit,
which strives to find meaning.
It seeks truth, not opinions.
It wants to know: What is happening in nature?
What is the highest good?

In the natural order of things, we find
the highest good, which is justice.
And when we follow our reason,
we become like ourselves.

We are all equal before God,
and no one is superior to another.
There is no difference between victory and defeat;
both are part of life's journey.

True nobility is not in wealth or status,
but in the ability to overcome difficulties.
No one should be ashamed of their circumstances;
it is how we respond that matters.

In the midst of joy and suffering, there is a difference:
I choose joy, for it is natural to us.
But when we face challenges, we must persevere.
We must put ourselves on equal footing with others,
one who is wealthy, another who is almost as fortunate.

They are equal in God's eyes;
it may seem strange, but that is how it is.

True wealth is not in external possessions,
but in the freedom to live our lives as we choose.
It is not the external world that brings us happiness,
but our inner state of being.

In the end, there are only two goods:
pain and pleasure. I would say that pain
is more valuable than pleasure.

To be able to endure joy without becoming corrupted
by it is a noble thing.
But to overcome suffering with courage
is even more admirable.

I hold in high esteem those who have shown bravery,
like Muций, who showed us an example of true nobility.
He was willing to sacrifice himself for the greater good,
and his act will be remembered for generations to come.

If you want to be famous, be healthy.
