# The Letter LXVII (On Courage)

I greet Seneca.

Let us begin with the weather - a strange spring,
And summer will not warm our rivers,
And I am chilled by old age's coldness.

As I read books, letters arrive
It seems I converse with you,
And together we find precise words,
And thoughts flow after them in a rush...

You ask: Is every good thing desirable?
Is the glory of being on fire?

I try to push aside joy, even for a year...
I pray that my spirit will not burn out...

There are only pure joys:
The peace of being surrounded by beloved books,
Not knowing sickness or hardship...

Who would not be drawn to such a blessing?

But virtue can be harsh and demanding,
And courage requires effort from us...

Know this: the symbol of courage is not words,
But action in moments of trial.

"But who has ever wanted such a thing?" 

I recall Caton and Socrates...
If I were to offer them another choice,
The path of courage would still be the right one.

Whoever endures trials with courage
Has all virtues as his shield:
Tolerance does not ask for mercy,
And reason on the fire does not flinch.

To be courageous is to be great,
To be chaste, I reject comfort...

There are blessings that bring joy and sorrow,
We show respect by bowing down to them.

Mark Regulus returned to prison, making a promise,
He did not beg for peace with his native Rome.

He was subjected to terrible torments again,
And died behind an enemy wall.

His fate was called "a peaceful sea"
Demetrius, who showed great courage.

Whoever has never known the spark of anger
Can say he lived before his time...

Light the fire! The glory of good things is great

I will add my own sparks to it...
The crackling of the flames - a victorious battle cry for my soul!

I burn with unbroken spirit...
