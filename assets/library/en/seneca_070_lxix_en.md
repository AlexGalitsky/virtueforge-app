**Letter LXIX (On the Firmness of the Spirit)**

Lucilia greets Seneca.

The unyielding spirit is called upon by our wanderings.

Until you stop your body from running,

Neither happiness nor bliss will come.

Health is only in prolonged treatment,

Life's former state is to forget peace.

Only by moving will you be awakened to enticements,

And desires will stir and disturb.

To those who are in love, they must free themselves from passion,

Their bodies need to forget their loved ones.

Thus, trying to tear away everything that was once happiness,

And the shock, and the rumor of death.

Gluttony's lust creates a hunger for money.

Any passion in the soul breeds rebellion:

Who is greedy - the reward is indulgence,

The virtuous person seeks his own company.

Vices tempt us as a reward,

And virtue enters our home without cost.

"Should we enslave ourselves to vices?... What joy?"

Only in perfection, which we have worked for.

Prepare yourself for death. 

Earlier, dying,

No one dies at the right time.

"No one chooses foreign death,"

You leave it - only foreign.

Be healthy.
