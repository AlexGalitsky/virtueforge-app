### Philosophical Context
In this letter, Seneca underscores the importance of life and its meaning, while raising questions about suicide and death. Written in verse, it is part of the "Letters from a Stoic" series, where Seneca explores various philosophical issues.

### Essence
- Seneca asserts that life is worth cherishing not for its duration but for its quality.
- He emphasizes the importance of being prepared for death and acknowledging its inevitable presence in life.
- Seneca also poses the question of which is more heinous: killing others or theft that allows one to die.
- The letter references the story of Socrates and his experiences in prison, serving as an example of wisdom and readiness for death.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca ties the idea of life's worth to virtue. He argues that one should live in such a way that it is worth living, not merely to prolong life. This requires wisdom and readiness for death.

### Question to Contemplate
How can one distinguish between theft that allows one to die and murder?
