**Letter LXXI (On Integrity)**

Lucilia greets Seneca!

You are asking for my counsel across the sea,

But life is faster than letters on a telegraph,

It rushes by and outpaces the sun's light.

I will give you advice not for a day or a year,

But one that you can follow all your life:

Look for true goodness in everything,

Mark your goal and hold onto it with all your might.

The main fault is that people see only parts,

And no one sees the whole picture.

A shot has been fired, but how to hit the mark?

When the entire target is invisible?

Without a goal, art is just scribbling,

Poets waste their time writing lines,

And sailors don't even know when they're sailing.

So where is the goal? You ask me that,

I will be brief, and it's well-known already,

What is hidden in virtue:

True goodness is not in external rewards,

But in being honest.

Be honest to everyone, not just your friends,

Even to your enemies, show mercy!

Then you won't be defeated by diseases,

And in trials, you'll be calmer than an executioner.

Whoever is honest, even if hungry and naked,

Won't sell the Prophet's treasure for silver.

All misfortunes will become blessings,

Because of honesty.

Let people say that our way of life is incompatible with happiness...

But whoever doesn't deceive anyone can feel sorry for it.

In all eternity, there is only one good:

Integrity, for those who look into their souls and not at the whip.

"Wisdom consists in distinguishing between right and wrong."

Socrates said so when talking about human nature.

Let people mock us: "You're gluttons!"

But we won't be ashamed of our struggle.

"A happy life is one that doesn't bend to fate."

Ask a young man what happiness means:

"My happiness is not being defeated by fate!"

To rise above the dead,

And speak words in the midst of the fallen.

The weight of troubles can't break a wise person,

A wise person carries their burdens without complaint,

Because they see them coming early in the morning,

What the day has to offer.

There are two parts to wisdom: the soul and the body,

He chooses the better one.

Let your body be broken by pain,

But remain calm and firm in spirit.

Don't think that, by nature, you're fighting against fate,

A wise person can break down even a wall with their words...

But the weight of troubles doesn't move them,

They yield to their fate and acknowledge themselves as slaves.

What I praise is myself for it,

But my spirit isn't strong enough to withstand,

The hardships of fortune,

And I'm willing to follow the path I love.

Sometimes, I even mock bravery,

When I say words about courage...

Wisdom doesn't color our souls,

It's a foundation for any motivation,

By which we breathe and live,

A firm foundation in true judgment

What I call virtue.
