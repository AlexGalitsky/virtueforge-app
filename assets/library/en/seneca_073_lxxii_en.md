# The Pursuit of Wisdom

Dear Seneca,

I once knew the answer to what you asked, but I have long since forgotten it. Like a book left unattended for a long time, I must stir myself up and reorganize my thoughts in order to find it again.

Forgive me if my response is delayed. There are things that can be understood in just two words, while others require more time to unravel. Some tasks may seem simple at first, but they turn out to be much more complex than we initially thought.

We sow the seeds of our own destiny, and from a small handful, we create something substantial. However, once we have sown it, we cannot simply abandon it. We must nurture it and care for it, just as we would tend to a garden.

For those who strive for wisdom, there is no time for idle pursuits. They must constantly work on themselves, eliminating the imperfections that hold them back.

Is there not enough time for true endeavors? But there is always time for frivolous activities.

"Everything is always getting in the way," they say.

So, why does the wise person remain joyful and content?

It is because perfection is within their grasp. Any sorrow or hardship they experience only serves to make them stronger.

Even when faced with the thought of death, it is merely a cold breeze that touches their skin. The true enemy is not death itself, but rather the suffering that comes with it.

What brings misery to one person may be a blessing to another. It is essential to understand this difference.

Our physical health is fleeting, but our inner well-being can last an eternity.

A healthy person is content and at peace, without striving for anything more.

The course of life is determined by fate, but we must not become complacent. We must continue to strive for excellence, even if it means falling short sometimes.

There are those who are almost healthy, but have not yet reached true wellness. They may be able to appreciate the blessings they receive, even if they do not fully understand them.

From this, we can learn that we should avoid getting entangled in obligations that will ultimately lead to our downfall. We must prioritize our own well-being and set boundaries to protect ourselves from harm.

Be healthy.
