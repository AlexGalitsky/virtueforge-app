# The Letter of LXXIV (On the Virtue of Good Fortune)

Lucilia greets Seneca!

Your letter has lifted me out of my lethargy:

I used to hide behind my eyelids,
Now I rush to dispel my doubts:

Do you believe that only virtue is good?

I will try again.

I take up my pen, like a knife, and am filled with longing
To help you without causing any harm.

Who has limited the scope of virtue?
That person alone possesses true happiness.

And he who still has something to gain,
Will not be defeated by his mind.

Some are tormented by children's illnesses,
Their troubles are endless.

Others are consumed by their love for a wife (is it her own or someone else's?)
Their passion is futile and burns like an open flame.

But the most numerous crowd
Are those who fear death, as if it were an enemy,

Who tremble with fear, stronger than they should be,

Life is more important to them than happiness.

Those who are poor in wealth are worse off than the rich,
Who have been struck by misfortune on a clear day

Did they die? Were they saved?
It's not the point, but the fear is noticeable.

Their salvation is justified by their laziness.

A lack of money, hypocrisy among women,
Public anger and envy - all trivialities...

The opportunity to suffer is more painful than our troubles,

How much we suffer depends on how much we have endured.

We don't run away from a blow,
But from the noise.

And we guard not our soul, but our body...

Can anyone be happy, I ask you?

Who lives in constant fear of what the future holds?

Who is guided by chance and arbitrary moods?

That person creates their own problems.

They watch as the scales of fate swing back and forth,

And wait anxiously to see what will happen next.

Let fortune play its deadly game,
Throwing away everything it has.

Some take more, while others lose,
But they are all trapped in this cycle.

The virtuous person is the only one who can break free

From the prison of their own desires.

What can be compared to eternity?
Is it not the truth that is eternal and sacred?

We lament that we have received so little,

And when we have, we soon lose it.

We are swept away by happiness,
And in our sorrow, we follow each other.

Blissful living is a lesson to us all,

A reminder of the importance of virtue.

Virtue is not just a facade or an appearance,
It is like the image of our Father.

It cannot be more virtuous than that.

Whether we live in a hut or a palace,

Our character remains unchanged.

Courage is not weakened by sorrow,

But rather strengthened.

The wise person is calm and confident,

Facing all challenges with ease.

Gluttony is dull and lazy,
Everyone praises them without doing anything.

But wisdom is swift and decisive,

Never losing its strength.

Fatigue comes to us when we are weak,
And those who have a strong spirit never tire.

Even if there are many days left before our death,
They are already preparing for it.

The prophecy of fate is a cruel mistress

Predicting poison, fires, and destruction...

But the only pain is what we feel today.

Why do we cry about the future?

Be healthy!

—
