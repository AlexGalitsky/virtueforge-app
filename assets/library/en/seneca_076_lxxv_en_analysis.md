### Philosophical Context
In this letter, Seneca emphasizes the importance of simplicity and sincerity in writing, distinguishing himself from a more ornate and oratorical style. He asserts that truth and feelings should be the primary focus of self-expression.

### Core
- Seneca underscores the importance of simplicity and sincerity in writing.
- He distinguishes between two types of people: those who strive for wisdom and knowledge, and those who seek fame and recognition.
- Seneca argues that a person who wishes to be in the first category should strive to expel vices and become unbreakable.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca connects simplicity and sincerity in writing to the importance of virtue, asserting that a person who strives to expel vices becomes unbreakable.
