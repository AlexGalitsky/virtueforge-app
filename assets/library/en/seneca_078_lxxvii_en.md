# Letter LXXVII (On the Readiness for Death)

Lucilius greets Seneca.

Today, ships have appeared,
Alongside them, a fleet with grain, as usual
For Rome's sake, the Egyptians have dredged.

They are noticeable in that they do not lower their small sail
When entering the harbor,
While others are forbidden to do so.
The young and old are distinguished by the veil of rain.

When ships enter Capri,
And we think of Minerva,
The wind dies down immediately.

Their sails are filled with the breeze,
" Messenger" overtakes them, forgive me...

The crowd rushes to the shore in anticipation
Of their arrival,
I follow them with pleasure:

There is no great arrival or loss,
In future letters, I do not find anything.

On the way, there is enough bread for a few days,
But what do I need on my own path?

More money than roads remain,
And that one who is not ready to die...

The journey has its goal and its goal is completion,
The road itself is just a journey.

Life cannot be imperfect,
Let it be short but honest.

Your Marcellinus was calm in youth,
He grew old suddenly, ill.

He summoned friends, only those worthy,
Like a man dressed for death.

One said it was hard to overcome his fear,
Another could not understand what the other wanted.

One Attal said something unusual:
Food, love, and sleep - that's your whole world...

No, Marcellinus, do not struggle as if
You were resolving very important matters:

Slaves and dogs wait outside the hut,
That's what's important to bring death to our doorstep.

He helped them by calming their fear,
And reminded Marcellinus to leave an inheritance "on a toothpick".

Everything went smoothly without bloodshed or violence,
They starved for three short days,
In a hot bath, in a sweet powerlessness,
He died, life easily changing into death.

I will call it cowardice, not otherwise,
That person who fears death and runs away,

Who does not know how to die?
Not living as if they were dying?

Do not forget: you are just one of many
Who have completed the path of life.

His soul is passing through, not his legs,
And his body will never be deceived by death.

You are waiting for me to bring examples of the Greats?

I will bring a child to your example:
A Spartan boy with courage on his face,
Said: "I am not a slave!" and accepted death...

Freedom does not accept cowardice:
It is only in those who give everything to it.

And, life with fear of death - that's also slavery,
You are just a tool, a means to an end,
You are just a drop, no matter how many kettles
You pass through the urinary bladder...

But the moment comes... you need resolve

To say "I forgive"...

What do you look for in a small window?
Friends? Homeland? Evaluating them? - No.

You have succeeded, you have extinguished yourself,
Like the sun...

What did you achieve worthy of light?

The Emperor is captured and asks for mercy:

"Please, you are revered by my favor..."

Are you still alive?
" I want to live! I am bound by life's obligations
I want to constantly follow them..."

Such a duty is also yours:
To give up your life for the three treasures

of family and close kin.

What is our life? - A story from ancient times...

Let it be short, was the end good?

Then the audience will remember with interest!

Think about the ending...

Be healthy.
