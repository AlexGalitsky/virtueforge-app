# Gratitude

Lucilia greets Seneca!

Is ingratitude met with surprise? Is it the first time in a person's lifetime?

Perhaps they were once cautious.

But caution does not lead to good here:

From baseless fears and insignificant worries,

Gratitude fades away like mist on the wind.

It is better not to find even one response,

Than to fail at doing something kind.

Years of drought will pass, and again a sower will rush into the field.

Perhaps we should meet gratitude occasionally, just once in a while.

A shot of kindness flies through the air.

Does clarity of purpose offend the wise?

(With more emphasis on punctuation) - it does not offend them.

Life stagnates in idle leisure,

For those whose path is guided by virtue.

Let us not be discouraged by failures... what's the point?

Try again, if you know what you're doing.

If someone has helped us but ruined everything...

Did they disappear? Or do we owe them something?

Perhaps the benefit was less than the harm,

The judge will recognize that both sides are equal.

Oscillations of anger are heavier than burdens,

But when someone does good before us... 

Taking into account time and circumstances,

We must understand: we owe nothing.

It is essential to know: did they help willingly?

Or perhaps it was an accident, maybe?

Sometimes knowing their intentions can help us forget the harm...

Whoever has done something kind will find gratitude in their heart.

Forgiveness of grudges... let them wither away,

Not harming others who have not offended.

A person who is not grateful is like a weed that needs to be pulled.

The worst thing about ingratitude... 

Is that it's always present, even when we're not aware of it.

"Evil gradually consumes itself"

When someone is consumed by their own evil...

They are slowly poisoning themselves with their own venom.

Ingratitude torments us with our own pain,

Trying to diminish our debt of kindness,

And fueling our anger and resentment,

Until we die... with a handful of silver.

A wise person does not seek only bad things in life,

But rather, they acknowledge the flaws of humanity,

Remaining calm and composed,

And kindness is burning brightly on their face.

To be grateful is to despise the world's superficiality,

To walk into exile, bearing our cross,

To shed blood and ignore comfort,

And do good... for our own sake.

Gratitude is not more valuable than anything else...

Until we get it.

But when we receive it from someone else,

We should reward them with kindness, too.

Those who are in debt will repay their loans,

But those who give generously will find gratitude in their hearts.

A person without gratitude has lost their mind,

When they do not pay back twice what they received.

Those who have been helped will always help others,

And those who are protected will be able to defend themselves,

Good deeds will come full circle,

And examples of evil will only serve as a pretext for revenge.

The more we cultivate gratitude,

The higher we will rise in our hearts:

For us, the profit is not in our pockets,

But in the act itself, when it's done well.

To be grateful is to give thanks from the heart,

Not just with words.

A person who keeps their honor intact...

Will pass through the storm of anger and contempt,

And emerge stronger on the other side.

When we receive something good,

We should not expect anything in return,

But rather, let it enrich us.

Whoever has done something kind to us...

Deserves our respect and gratitude.

The more we cultivate kindness,

The more we will rise above ourselves:

For us, the reward is not in our pockets,

But in the act itself, when it's done well.

Those who are grateful will always be blessed,

And those who are ungrateful will wither away like a weed.

A person without gratitude is like a weed that needs to be pulled.

The worst thing about ingratitude... 

Is that it's always present, even when we're not aware of it.
