### Philosophical Context
In this letter, Seneca emphasizes the importance of gratitude and its opposite, ingratitude. He asserts that gratitude is the key to virtue and happiness.

### Core Message
- Gratitude is superior to evil as much as the noonday sun is brighter than the morning light.
- Ingratitude is a folly that persists for centuries everywhere.
- Gratitude does not fade in the wind and can be found even in seemingly trivial and insignificant circumstances.
- A wise person will measure what they receive appropriately and repay good deeds to the best of their ability.

### Connection to Virtue
Gratitude is linked to the virtues of honesty and fidelity. Seneca argues that a wise person loves gratitude and fidelity, whereas the wicked person is only interested in evil.

### Question for Reflection
As we ascend in the soul, how often will gratitude accompany us?
