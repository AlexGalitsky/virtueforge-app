### Philosophical Context
Letter LXXXIII (On Drunkenness) is part of Seneca's philosophical works, one of the most renowned Stoics in the history of philosophy. This text offers a critical assessment of drunkenness and its impact on the individual, emphasizing the importance of restraint in this regard.

### Core Message
- Drunkenness is a vice that should be avoided.
- Drunkenness weakens the mind and leads to senselessness and unpredictability.
- Wine is an enemy that undermines the fortress and reason of a person.
- The wise person does not get drunk and does not die from poison.
- Restraining from drunkenness is a key aspect of the life of a wise person.

### Connection to Virtue
Drunkenness is linked to the virtue of honesty, as it weakens the mind and leads to senselessness. Restraining from drunkenness is a key aspect of the life of a wise person, who demonstrates their honesty and restraint.
