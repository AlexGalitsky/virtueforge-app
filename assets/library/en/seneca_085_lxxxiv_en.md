# The Letter LXXXIV (On Reading and Writing)

Lucilius greets Seneca!

Sometimes journeys invigorate me:

When there is no one to converse with,

I read, and my journey does not tire me.

When I write, I exhaust myself.

But reading relaxes me. It takes its turn

And occupies me: in translations, dear friend,

Neither laziness nor fatigue can capture me.

We should emulate the industrious bees,

Collecting honey without cease from all the flowers around,
And guiding their flight with a cheerful hum to the thousands.

Although we do not know for certain:

Do they gather honey, or make it from the nectar
That they collect all around the countryside?

We eat honey, knowing nothing about it.

Take books into your journey and try them out,

Mix them up, create a single flavor,
To soothe your soul.

Just as eating first fills our stomachs,
And then gives us strength,

Reading enters our minds,
And suddenly presents us with our own thoughts.

The food we eat only nourishes our bodies,

But the knowledge we acquire develops our minds.

Let your heart conceal its deepest desires,

For they will manifest in the results of your thoughts.

May there be harmony in your views, like the osancks...

Not a copy, but an echo of the father's likeness:

A great mind leaves its mark on everything it touches.

When you quote others, do not avoid criticism:

Foreign thoughts are the music of foreign hours...

I want to hear the power of your chorus,

Not the sound of individual voices.

How can one sing?—Listen to reason:

Leave wealth, envy, and fame,
For they have no end, and we reach them too soon.

Look up: there is always someone else there...

Do not climb high mountains,

Keep the choirs of the wealthy at a distance.

Go ahead on the simple path, with gentle slopes,

And do not divert your eyes from wisdom.

The uneven path leads to the peaks of worldly life,
For all those seeking living gifts...

But reason cannot be measured by that yardstick,

Go ahead on the simple path.

Be healthy.
