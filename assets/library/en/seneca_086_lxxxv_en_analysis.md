### Philosophical Context
This text is a letter from Seneca in which he critiques the Peripatetics and their views on life. Seneca argues that the Peripatetics oversimplify the concepts of vice and virtue.

### Essence
- Stoics believe that a rational person should be moderate in their desires and actions.
- Peripatetics, on the other hand, hold that vice and virtue can only be understood through passions and emotions.
- Seneca asserts that wisdom lies not in avoiding passions but in managing them and not allowing them to control one’s actions.
- Stoics maintain that virtue is the foundation of human well-being, but that perfect virtue is unattainable.

### Connection to Virtue
Stoics regard virtue as the most crucial aspect of human life, but they also believe it cannot be idealized. Seneca argues that virtue must be practical and real, not merely theoretical.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us can truly manage their passions and not let them dominate our actions?
