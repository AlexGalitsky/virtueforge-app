# The Letter LXXXVI (On the Bath of Scipio)

Lucilius greets Seneca!

In Literary Africa, Scipio

He ended his life, leaving Rome forever:

He voluntarily chose exile.

The great victor of Hannibal,

At the peak of his power he left authority,

When he thought it was not befitting Rome

To have him near.

" I shall not change anything in the laws,

Take my gift, beautiful gift of love:

My power becomes a burden,

I retire - live free."

One of two things had to happen:

Freedom with an alien will did not reside...

The spirits of greatness are to be studied,

Those sitting in power are dead men.

I have felt the manes and his tomb,

Whose altar was destroyed.

And I compared how we live, my dear,

To the greatness of our ancestors who lived before us.

Here, in this dark bath, narrow, windowless,

The leader washed his body and earthy dust,

His honest labor, hard but lawful,

Ended... Like a living being before me...

Now we have an Alexandrian marble,

Circles on the walls, a roof of glass,

Water flows from precious faucets,

Bodies are immersed.

And how many columns and decorations,

All shine with luxury...

And the sound of water flowing down the stairs,

And the beautiful color underfoot...

But in this bath, instead of windows - shutters

The light barely breathes...

We wanted to bask in them,

And see the sea...

There is no benefit in it.

Before they were not decorated,

Why, when their value was zero?

Water did not flow, nor did anyone stir...

To clean the dirt, the purpose of the bath alone.

But, how pleasant it is to be here with one who knows,

That we washed here Scipio and Caton.

Now, however, they are heating up the sun

What can torment us, if there's something to do...

What is life in a village for Scipio?

He washed himself with unfiltered water,

Which washed away the ilt between the roots...

Here is an old man's tale:

Only his hands and feet were washed daily,

But his body was washed - eight days apart.

Now they wash more often, without doubt...

But people are getting dirtier... No, cleaner...

Horace described in satires:

"The scent of Bucillus, a man with a donkey's back,

The smell of Gorgonius, a man with a goat's head..."

He condemned Bucillus not by smell...

Is it time to tell the story of ancient life?

Now the estate is ruled by Egius:

He can plant old trees,

And taught me what he knew.

Vergilius wrote beautifully about everything,

Trying to heal us with beauty...

But was not always truthful,

And I must correct him:

"I sow wheat and oats in spring..."

I write in June: I met people:

Some gather wheat in baskets,

Others sow oats... on the same day.

Two ways to plant olives:

By leaving a branch on foot,

They make a mound of manure, patiently

Planting them.

They cut off the short trunk of the root:

In spring you see the dense foliage,

And the winds do not cool it down...

The roots grow stronger, and they will flourish.

Another example of planting - take a stem

With soft skin, just like a leaf.

Though slower to grow, smooth as reeds;

Strong - frost cannot harm them.

I conclude my teaching,

Or else I would surpass all masters...

And what about me? - Only a competitor,

Like Egius...
