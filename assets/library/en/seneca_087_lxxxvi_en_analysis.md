### Philosophical Context
Seneca's letter to Lucilius, written in the Stoic style, delves into themes of freedom, power, and the human condition. The text reflects on the life and death of Scipio Africanus, a Roman general who voluntarily went into exile to live a simple life in the countryside.

### Essence
- In this letter, Seneca emphasizes the importance of freedom and simplicity in life.
- He contrasts the life of Scipio with that of those who sit on thrones, highlighting the difference between true freedom and power.
- Seneca also mentions public opinion and cultural heritage, underscoring the importance of remaining true to one's principles even in the face of societal pressure.
- The letter also references classical philosophers such as Euhemerus and Vergilius.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca connects simplicity and freedom to virtue, stressing the importance of staying true to one's principles in life. This aligns with the idea that virtue is the foundation for achieving freedom and happiness.

### Question to Ponder
Why do people often succumb to power and societal pressure rather than striving for simplicity and freedom?
