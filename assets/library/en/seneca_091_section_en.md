**The Letter of CS (On Philosophy and Crafts)**

Lucilia greets Seneca!

From God - philosophy and life.

Dressed in the armor of philosophy

To understand: How should he live?

Like night piercing the sun at dawn,

Philosophy brings clarity to the world.

Let it not be given to us from birth our wisdom,

But from God, we have the ability to attain it.

Imagine if all were born wise

To what purpose do we strive in the chaos of days?

Is not the alchemy of obligation worthless?

Achieved through effort alone.

For one thing: searching for essence

In all human and divine endeavors,

To walk towards goodness through trials and tribulations,

And not be intimidated by the specter of danger.

She teaches us to worship God - in Him lies all beginnings.

Teaches us to love humanity.

The community of humans would not have ceased to exist,

But alchemy "helped" it forget.

Remember, there were first mortals among men,

Living in harmony with nature,

And those who ruled best lived on after death,

Having outwitted their fellowmen.

Indeed, subjugation to a better being,

Is found in nature and animals, and humans.

A leading the herd of cattle is not the most noble...

And humans need a king, not a wrongdoer.

They chose leaders based on their inner qualities,

And lived happily in their communities:

For them, concern for their neighbors was work at that time.

Only the wise could sit on the throne

In an era called the Golden Age.

It was not about the crown,

But about the head, without the emptiness of greed.

The weak were protected by the strong,

Teaching us what is useful and what is not.

They did not feel tyranny,

But rather virtues that shone like a magical light.

Then sin appeared in humanity,

And wise men devised laws.

Seven sages were famous in that era,

Athenians founded by the sage Solon.

Zaleucus from Locri, and Lycurgus from Sparta,

Cataneeus from Sicily, Charondas...

They managed to establish both law and right without debating different factions.

Let it be said: "The philosopher invented science and crafts..."

Do not require my agreement:

"The peacock gloats over its plumage, but the eagle does not."

"Do philosophers build houses?"

For me, the sign of opulence is in their homes.

Thus, alchemy destroyed the strength of Troy

(Through Helen, and then... a horse).

Then an architect appeared,

To build palaces with grandiose projects,

Including a golden ceiling.

Before that, people saved branches from rain,

Arranged correctly on the roof.

And they were not afraid that the wind would blow them away...

Now under white marble, they tremble.

It was not the philosophers who invented ironworking

Or began to hammer and shape it:

What is clever, perhaps, is useful?

Is not worth calling wise.

"What came first: The hammer? The anvil?"

A question not interesting to me...

For the wise person does not need things.

Why should he burden himself with them?

Who do you think is wiser today?

Those who invented a whimsical fountain,

And changing tablecloths with changing patterns?

Or those who can show others how to live?

What is not needed for life: work and war?

The world was created for our convenience,

And every encounter with another person is like a brother.

Needless darkness does not know trouble,

But joy has many labors.

The fur of the lynx protects us from wind,

As well as from winter's cold.

It is easier to escape summer's heat:

In ancient times, there were plenty of caves in the mountains.

A simple awning was enough for no one to complain,

You would find everything you needed around you.

Animals live easily in nature,

Without knowledge of crafts and sciences...

But man has created a complicated circle

Of troubles and needs that he himself created.

He has surpassed himself with his desires,

And lost the natural limit.

The art of craftsmanship is chaos,

Everything is for our own use,

What we call "need".

We are slaves to our desires,

And have forgotten our true nature.

God gave us the ability to be wise,

Not the ability to be rich.

The earth was richer then,

When it was not touched by human hands.

Those who loved themselves, and not others,

Did not make war against wild beasts.

They found refuge in simple dwellings,

And were at peace with nature.

Their beds were not under vaulted ceilings,

But they slept peacefully on the ground.

No one among them was corrupted by wealth,

Or did anything to harm his neighbor.

It was not for humans that they made weapons,

But against wild and greedy animals.

They found refuge in the wilderness,

And were more peaceful than we are.

Their hearts were pure, and untainted by nature,

And they lived in harmony with the earth.

The roots of virtue lie elsewhere.

They did not know silver or precious colors,

Or gold without alloy...

I would call this life "eternal summer",

But now I have lost its keys.

Nature is the mother,

They are her good children...
