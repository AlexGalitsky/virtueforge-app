# The Misfortunes of Life

Lucilla greets Seneca!

Misfortune has befallen Liberalias: a fire
left only scars in one night

No city has ever suffered from the flames
without its buildings being consumed.

Where was the beauty of Gaul?
Even Hannibal, the great conqueror,
could not erase it from memory.

I write longer about the destruction
of this glorious city than I did about its fall.

Sometimes unexpectedness is more crushing
than any other blow.

For it is when our fate turns against us
that we are most weighed down by sorrow.

We must therefore be prepared for
any manifestation of fate:

for every misfortune that has come to pass,
there will be another. No oaths, no prayers can save us.

Fortune does not promise safety:
war breaks out in the world,
a clear day turns into a dark night,
and even justice is obstructed by obstacles.

Good gives way to evil, and instead of honey,
poison flows from the wounds of the people.

If there is no cause for our downfall,
then it is our own excess of happiness that brings about our ruin.

In an hour, empires are overthrown,
the flow of fate breaks through the walls,
and the strong are brought down by the weak.

Gentle growth is not enough;
fortune turns a blind eye to our efforts.

Countries that have known joy and prosperity
are long since nothing but dust.

Let us keep in mind all the misfortunes
that have befallen humanity:
murders, catastrophes, and wars.

Do not look forward to what you see often,
but rather to what you dare not even imagine.

How many cities have been destroyed by earthquakes?
Even Paphos was reduced to ashes.

Why do we build again and again, only
to have it all taken away?

Time will erase the traces of all that is great.

All falls apart: cities, mountains, and seas...

And everything is nothing but dust. This, I swear, is not a coincidence.

This is how... (it's just an excuse to comfort myself)

I ask you to believe that every misfortune
will eventually pass, but fate will bring us even greater trials.

The infamous Timagenes, who was once celebrated,
said: "Fires in Rome are so painful because the ashes
are more bitter than the flames."

May your spirit understand and endure your fate:

Fortune can do anything, in her power.

And even those who have risen to the highest heights
will fall, just like the weak.

Those born of equal birth will die equally.
The king of Macedon, Alexander, was a tragic figure,
who suddenly realized that his hard work was futile...

He asked his teacher for advice:
"There are things that are equally difficult."

So is fate: it crushes us with its weight
and forces us to grow old.

But you can ease your own suffering:

live quietly and die easily.

As Demetrius said:
"Words of malice are like the sounds of the body,
and I often fail to notice where they come from."

You cannot be ashamed of things that are shameful.
Only those who spread evil words in public
should feel shame.

A good man thinks only of what is important,
and his enmity is not based on empty talk.

Even death has a bad reputation,
but it is the doctor's medicine that cures us.

It is not for anyone to take the reins before me.

Be healthy.
