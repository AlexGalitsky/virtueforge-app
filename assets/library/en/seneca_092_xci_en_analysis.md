### Philosophical Context
Letter XCI is part of Seneca's philosophical work, one of the most significant Stoics. In this letter, he addresses the theme of misfortune and its connection to fate, emphasizing the importance of preparedness for encountering unexpected events.

### Essence
- Letter XCI underscores the importance of preparedness for encountering unexpected events.
- Seneca asserts that fate can be harsh and unjust, but we can be prepared for its consequences.
- He also highlights the importance of calmness and ease in life as a means to alleviate suffering.

### Form of This Version
Letter XCI is written in prose.

### Connection to Virtue
The connection to virtue lies in the idea that preparedness for encountering unexpected events is a key aspect of character and moral development. This principle aligns with the principle of justice and equality that underpins Stoic philosophy.

### Question to Ponder
Who among people can be prepared for any event, and how might they alleviate suffering?
