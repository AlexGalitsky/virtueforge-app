### Philosophical Context
In this letter, HSP (Lucilius) addresses Seneca with questions about the blissful life and virtue. The letter presents a dialogue between two philosophers discussing the nature of happiness, wisdom, and virtue.

### Essence
- Happiness is not found in transient trifles but in its immunity to sorrow and suffering.
- Wisdom is the ability to avoid madness and maintain integrity under any circumstances.
- Virtue is what keeps us above madness and preserves wisdom.
- Happiness cannot be added to or subtracted from life; it is a natural outcome of living in harmony with oneself and the world around us.

### Connection to Virtue
Virtue is the foundation for achieving happiness. As with Franklin Weeks' Order/Chastity/etc., virtue is the key to maintaining wisdom and avoiding madness.

### Question to Ponder
Who can truly claim to be happy if they have not experienced pain or suffering?
