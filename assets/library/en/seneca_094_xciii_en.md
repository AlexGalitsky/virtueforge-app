# The Letter XCIII (On the Length of Life)

Dear Seneca,

You lament that Metronactus has died too soon.

Has he not yet reached the end of the race?

Life is a play with death as its final act.

Be just, and submit to nature's will

For it is already predetermined from birth.

But how can one lift one's spirit in life?

This is the concern of wise men.

Do not condemn the completed span of life,

And do not be quick to anger at fate's decree.

How long does one live? - It depends on chance...

And, how long does one live abundantly? - Only from the heart

Behold, a man who has lived eighty years in idleness...

Did he truly live? Or did he merely wither away?

The answer to the length of life will be different,

For man is not a tree or a coral reef.

I wish that life, like precious metals,

Would be valued by weight, not by size,

By deeds, not by time,

What death has inscribed on the tombstone...

Let us praise those who have lived happily,

Who have seen the light and lived against fate:

Who have run through its tempests, its flood,

And left behind a memory of themselves!

Do not ask me when I was born.

For life has given me everything,

And death has set me free,

I now look at it calmly.

How long will I live? - It is unknown to me,

But whether they will remember me - that depends on me.

Life is an easy thing against a virtuous life

I would not have been otherwise placed or changed.

Blessed is the man who has achieved wisdom in life:

There is no higher goal, and no greater gift!

You have extended your lifespan beyond the boundaries of gold,

The whole world will see what is true to you.

We have enjoyed the knowledge of nature:

The movement of the stars, and the sun, and the moon

Around the earth, year by year,

And its rhythm has ruled for centuries.

A wise man departs - not to the blessed,

But with his soul among them.

Even if I disappear completely...

My courage will not be shaken.

The praise is small, but worthy:

For in it, wisdom and benefit live together...
