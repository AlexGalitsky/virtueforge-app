### Philosophical Context
Letter XCIII by Seneca is a response to the question of how long one should live and how to "live enough." This text reflects the philosophical idea that life is not solely measured by the duration of one's existence but also by the spiritual state and actions of the individual.

### Essence
- Life has a predetermined end.
- Death is the final act of life's drama.
- One should not judge others based on the time they have left to live.
- The length of one's life is determined by their spirit.
- A person who lives a virtuous life will be remembered long after they are gone.
- Virtue is the highest goal and reward.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Life is like a play with a predetermined ending, but it is not just about the amount of time one has left to act; it is also about how one uses their time and spirit.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca emphasizes the importance of living in accordance with nature and seeking wisdom. This aligns with the idea that virtues are the primary direction of human life, not merely the quantity of time one has lived.

### Question to Ponder
How can one live "a life in abundance" without being attached to the duration of their life?
