Lucilia greets Seneca!

There are people who in philosophy take  
Only private advice for a man,  
The essence is for them a fruitless task.  
But the Stoic Ariston, quite the opposite,  
Thought aimless to pursue the particular:  
"He who knows the nature of good will not stray,  
And in particulars will see the true light  
In the art of javelin throwing, a skilled warrior  
Can hit any target,  
Who understands all, in any deed  
Can see the true light of good.  
Who considers instruction superfluous,  
Says: to teach the blind is useless,  
As if to teach the sighted,  
He can cure the blind himself.  
Such delusions blind the soul,  
And she lives in aimless wandering.  
What is the point of explaining to the deaf,  
How to listen?  
To the lepers, the thought of cleanliness?  
To the poor, the skill of being rich?  
To the hungry, the virtue of abstinence?  
First, we must satisfy their hunger,  
Before teaching them unnecessary nonsense.  
The same applies to vices:  
Until false beliefs are expelled,  
No one listens... to the sage, the prophet,  
Though they see him every day from their window.  
Let him know that in money there is neither good nor evil...  
Let him know how unhappy the rich are...  
Make him stop crying from fear  
Of patience and perseverance teach.  
When, having implanted the principles,  
You teach him to see the purpose of life,  
There is no need to follow him in particulars:  
For virtue is the compass of all deeds.  
Who feels virtue in his heart,  
To him health, fame - all is the same.  
(The vain philosophers preach,  
What was written long ago in the textbooks.)  
And, further, who needs advice,  
Even in clear or doubtful matters?  
If it is clear - there is no place for admonitions...  
Doubtful? - Who believes on an empty stomach?  
You bring evidence without measure,  
But the listener's soul is not touched...  
Stronger than evidence are examples,  
Therefore - they suffice alone.  
How to deal with people? - Justly!  
And, there is no need to teach the learned!  
The one who knows the essence - lives happily,  
The ignorant - give them the key to truth:  
Idle talk has stuffed their ears,  
They do not know the path of good and evil...  
And, who has it, will hardly listen  
To those who speak - whether from a throne or an ass.  
There are two reasons for our crimes:  
Wickedness, and the path to it - debauchery...  
There is no benefit here from private admonitions,  
The one who is deceived - is pleased with appearances.  
For each, in every case in life,  
You cannot find an admonition...  
The life of the wise - an example for all in the homeland,  
Though that example... is contrary to wickedness.  
What is obsession, what is the vice of opinions  
Is there a difference in what to admonish them?  
To drive out madness - to embark on persecution...  
Madness - to admonish the mad."  

...

Thus, Ariston thought, the great Stoic...  
And, to give him a worthy answer,  
It is necessary to examine everything separately,  
Then we will understand: where - truth, where - not.  
Agreed: sight cannot be restored by advice...  
Having restored sight, can he help others?  
A good character can do this,  
He drives evil from souls, as the sun drives night.  
And the doctor, not only heals, but teaches:  
Take care of your restored eyes.  
Their bright light hurts and tortures,  
To run out of darkness into light is impossible.  
Though the advice itself is too weak,  
But... it refreshes the memory of sins.  
Encouragement and consolation are not superfluous...  
And the best admonitions are in verse.  
Why teach the sick as if they were healthy?  
And where will you not find illness? - Show me!  
Sometimes the immortal has no roof over his head,  
And cannot find it... (eternal pauper)  
"Drive out greed - there is no need to admonish  
How to live, neither the rich nor the poor."  
Not only the desire for money - is an obstacle to good,  
Money should be spent for good.  
"Admonitions are powerless against vice."  
True, not always does the doctor heal the disease,  
But, he always heals a cold on time,  
And alleviates useless weeping.  
"Why do we preach the obvious?"  
To remind is to encourage!  
"There was a bribe here - everyone knows it...  
What is there to talk about?"  
You know: it is dishonorable to expect chastity  
In a wife, while you roam elsewhere...  
To care for a friend is holy...  
But, these thoughts - like ripples on water.  
"Without evidence, what is the point of admonitions?"  
Take a half-line from the "Aeneid",  
Take Cato, take a saying from Silius  
The soul freezes in silent contemplation...  
Like a spark, from the lightest breath,  
Releases its fire into freedom,  
And virtue - waits for a touch,  
Rises in a person, only if you touch it.  
But, if admonitions are useless,  
Only nature should be relied upon:  
Some are wise, but put their wisdom on the shelf,  
Others - only "put" their body on the bed.  
"To those who do not know the foundations,  
Admonitions do not help to cure vice."  
The seeds of good are in everyone from birth,  
As long as evil has not crossed the threshold.  
"He is bound by false beliefs in good and evil?  
Give him the truth, the details are unnecessary."  
No: the point is to do what is required.  
And what is required? - Explain to him.  
Everything is connected: principles, admonitions...  
In their path, virtue is the limit.  
Principles - first, there is no doubt,  
But, admonitions - also have their place.  
"From a mass of admonitions - disorder."  
Such an opinion is close to the scoundrel:  
The list of essential admonitions is short,  
Not distinguishing between place and person.  
"Wisdom does not impress the mad...  
Nor the wicked." - Is it so? Everywhere?  
Let criticism not heal them,  
But forces them to follow the rein.  
"Laws are admonitions with threats,  
And they cannot overcome evil."  
Who are frightened by future storms?  
Admonition is a sweetmeat, not a whip.  
Are principles needed for laws,  
What left us the good man Plato?  
Poseidonius tried to refute them...  
Does he not need them? - But, not everyone is like him.  
There is benefit in laws - in the law of right,  
(Though they are not beneficial for everyone...)  
A bad law is a guarantee of a bad character,  
Loopholes in it - success for vice.  
And, similarly, philosophy is beneficial,  
As a path to the development of mind and soul.  
And those who are hardened in their illnesses,  
What to criticize? - They have their own path: to sin...  
Virtue worries about the wicked,  
As the oar pulls a heavy load.  
There is such a thing in a great man,  
That has a beneficial effect without words.  
Fedon wrote: "The bites of insects  
Cannot be seen, though they suck blood."  
Even if you are not acquainted with the wise,  
Their advice will be beneficial to you.  
"Know yourself!" and "Nothing in excess!"  
Who asks: Why? - In that is the gift of the wise...  
I could give more examples  
Of great phrases, short as a blow.  
Instruction can help people,  
If criticism can shame them,  
For admonition is a gentle cure  
Of the soul, to free the path to good.  
The light of truth is called inspiration:  
We believe in ourselves, strengthen our soul.  
There are no obstacles for us, ready for daring  
To fly... to splash blood on the road.  
Agrippa did not push others to misfortune,  
But helped them to survive in misfortune:  
"Even the small will rise with agreement,  
The great - discord cannot overcome."  
"Why a stream of advice, of condolences,  
For those who are not naturally foolish?"  
Admonitions are necessary,  
In the struggle with the false opinion of the crowd.  
Wishing good or cursing  
Do equally harm...  
Often they desire what they do not know,  
And false fear they instill, leaving.  
No one strays alone,  
He shares his vices with another,  
We do not heal the vices of epidemics,  
On the contrary, the crowd sings hymns to them!  
Vices are praised with skill,  
No word is heard about the struggle...  
In the end - crushing baseness,  
That everyone sees in others... not in themselves...  
Let the guardian take us by the ear,  
And drown out the noise of the crowd with a shout,  
Let him, as a source of moral hearing,  
Order to turn their feet towards him.  
Nature does not push us to vice:  
All gold is in the earth, there - and diamonds  
Trample! No, we gain little in the general good...  
And, for diamonds, we are ready to trample.  
Nature raises our faces to the sky,  
To observe the beautiful course of the stars.  
But man, not thinking: "This is for me...",  
Again lowers his eyes to the ground.  
He seeks gold there (at his own expense),  
Seeks iron (the cliffs are like cliffs),  
And soon sees death, following them,  
The cause and instrument of the fall.  
Look at the miners: their hands  
Soaked to the fingertips in dirt.  
But in their souls - more suffering from metals:  
Where are the rich who can live laughing?  
Do not covet recognition, the symbol of power,  
Do not reach for wealth with your hands:  
The rich are not in control and are unhappy.  
Do you want power? - Drive out vices.  
There will be those who, seeing the phantom of glory,  
Will set fire to walls, cities...  
Who will conquer great empires,  
Which they have never seen,  
No one dares to look into their eyes,  
Another's life is not valuable to them.  
Driven by... ambition and cruelty.  
Only an appearance: they push their enemies.  
The vain Alexander, driven by passion  
To destruction, overthrew Greece  
(where he was raised). No one in their right mind,  
Who was intoxicated with power, and climbed over corpses.  
Athens, Sparta, he conquered when he was young...  
It would seem, everything was going well for him!  
Like a predator, feeling no hunger,  
Eating carefree prey.  
Ready to commit violence against nature,  
To establish greatness over it...  
But, a stone, jumping over waves,  
Eventually sinks, and the end of the journey - at the bottom.  
And Gnaeus Pompey, from false greatness,  
Waged wars, prolonging his power...  
And Caesar, imagining his superiority  
Above all, saw pleasure in elevation.  
He could not tolerate a rival,  
Though the country could tolerate two...  
"Brutus! Even you?"... and the body cooled  
Of the once unreachable eagle.  
Gaius Marius - also, consul so many times,  
That it cannot be counted, to the detriment of fellow citizens...  
Do not believe that Marius led the army into battle,  
No - Marius' ambition led him.  
Like tornadoes, tearing everything apart,  
And leaving the city in ruins...  
So he, who brought only misfortune,  
And himself, believe me, found no happiness.  
In none of them do we find wisdom,  
Who did they help, even a drop?  
All wisdom - in returning to nature,  
From where vices have taken us.  
Let us leave the instigators of vice,  
Crowding together, infecting each other.  
Without an audience - even gold is useless,  
Only a mirror reflects it.  
No one dresses in luxury  
To amaze themselves and their loved ones.  
Our admirer - a devoted helper...  
To display his madness to the people.  
Do not be captivated by signs of distinction  
On a height that inspires envy:  
There they look into the abyss of their greatness,  
Shuddering: "How to stay on the horse?"  
There are many philosophers out of fear  
Reason woke up in them, sensing blood...  
Do not forget: in successes - the shadow of failure,  
In adversity - reason awakens.  
Be well.
