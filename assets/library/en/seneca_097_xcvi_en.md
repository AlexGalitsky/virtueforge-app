# The Letter of CXCVI (On the Trials of Fate)

Dear Seneca,

I notice that you are currently troubled.

There is only one misfortune in a person when they are consumed by it while sitting.

Are you ill? Is it time to pay taxes?

Do your debts torment you? Does your home feel like an exile's?

Losses and illness? Affairs? Fears?

Everything collapses, which was hard-won.

I want to tell you something secret:

The inability to see and the enmity I am striving for God openly,

I do not submit - with my whole soul I go.

By the will of goodness I weep taxes,

Even when hunger often whispers: Hold on!

What pain and anger instill in many

Are not our misfortunes, but "tax on life".

In those days when you wanted to see old age,

Did you know what was glorious then?

All travelers on the big road have received:

snow, rain, and dirt, dust, wind, and heat.

You wanted to live, forgetting the unpleasant?

That woman came to you - how could you?!

From my heart I command you against fortune's favor:

May God deliver you from luck!

While some wander through darkness with caution,

Others neglect their bodies, like birds.

Their shame is a cure for them.

So do not live like that, Senecio,

Our life is akin to military service,

Our eternal battle is merciless and harsh.

I hope no explanation is needed?

Do not avoid danger.

Be healthy.
