# The Letter of CXCVII (On Crime and Punishment)

Dear Seneca,

You think, Lucilius, that vice,
Is a luxury confined to our age?

We pay for it in full measure.

It is not times, but men, with the thought: "What's next?"

They indulge, saying "hah-ha..."

To acknowledge shame: in front of Cato

The most heinous crimes are committed.

Pulcherius Claudius was accused of secret adultery

With Pompey (that was Caesar's wife...)

He paid off judges with money?

His own sin was the price of redemption:

Those judges, besides money, received

Youths, women - everything was part of the bribe...

Imagine how those judges distinguished themselves...

Who else could be brought to justice?!

Offering bribes in exchange for adultery

Is more sinful than committing it.

Not out of a desire to protect their family,

By covering up their sin, they went astray.

Cato asked: "Why do you need protection?"

So that the bribe wouldn't be taken away from the court?

The adulterer and the procurer...it's strange...

"Lawfully" justified forever.

Tell me: are there natures more depraved than this?!

(I am tormented by the consequences...)

A crime of cunning was committed,

Which led to all the consequences...

In Cato's presence, they stripped off their clothes

The people didn't demand harsh punishment for these shameless women...

Their promiscuity opened up all its parts,

And they didn't receive any severe retribution.

This kind of reception is known to many people,

A lawsuit was started with them:

So that they wouldn't acknowledge themselves guilty in adultery,

Judges were bribed to mar their reputation.

Oh, Claudius - you are always cunning...

Only occasionally does a Cato appear...

There are those who live with vices so deeply ingrained,

That they lead others astray, not away from it.

Those who profit from the downfall of others,

Don't rejoice in their misfortune,

And the lawyer who buried his own sin...

The murderer, thief, and adulterer don't cry...

As long as vice carries them on its wings...

You'll find "disappointed" people who have succeeded,

But not "disappointed" people who have failed.

And those who are deeply corrupted by evil...

Still feel a sense of goodness.

They hide their sins away...

Can fear overcome sin?

That's what Epicurus said: "Thieves can hide

But they cannot be sure!"

I add (the meaning will become clearer):

You cannot forget about crime.

Even if it's not punished publicly,

The punishment for wrongdoing is in itself:

Success is a debt of gratitude,

Fear of failure is sent as a letter...

Don't agree with Epicurus,

That sin can be cured by fear alone.

But if fear lives in human nature,

Nature saves us from sin.

Nature replaces punishment with fear:

A thunderclap has passed, but you hear the rumble

From heaven in your soul, like an omen of doom,

Conscience accuses us.

Be healthy.
