# Letters of the 1st Century (Fragments of Two Letters)

Lucilius greets Seneca!

Dependent on happiness is unhappy.

Happiness passes like escapes of the dead

Wonderful olive trees bear fruit

"What can one enjoy happiness?"

Perhaps not dependent on it.

Does fate deserve our happiness?

Seek its source in your soul.

A bad soul leads to a bad end,

And to worse, beyond the limits of reason.

In direct souls there is a firm foundation,

That does not give itself to fate's blows

Fortune - and modest, and grateful,

In misfortunes - an example of strength.

The mind is calm, given by God,

Not believing in promises of chimera.

Look at others (here it shines brighter),

Even yourself - where can chance help us?

Do not seek the other's anger in loss,

Say to yourself: "Otherwise God judges."

When you deceived your expectations,

And people want to express outrage...

Do not hurry, proud creation,

Say to yourself: "God judges more truly!"

Who is so set up that he does not complain about anything.

But how can he acquire such a disposition?!

While everything is going well, it's easier

Than to build a strong structure through bitter suffering.

Who sees their future as ominous

Will not survive misfortune.

Fear losing your wife and possessions

Is the price of losing them, so that you will grieve.

No, I do not preach carelessness:

What is frightening - try to avoid,

But there is something more foolish and inhuman

Than seeing misfortunes in one's own shadow.

Everything happens from a lack of measure:

Some people wait for misery all their lives,

Others believe in happiness as in a chimera,

Not believing that... when they will fall.

"Any good of mortal life is equally mortal!"

You can't say it more clearly (this is Metrodor).

One only virtue remains unchanged

In it lies the essence of reliable moral support.

But people are so shameless that they are surprised

By every loss...

Who himself is weak, how did he believe

That all rights to property are strong?!

"What then should I call on for help?"

Leave your possessions their fruit:

No one will take what you remember,

Remember in the past good deeds.

There's no terror, and death has its delight...

I want to bring examples to you:

Here Muysis is fire, and the poison of Socrates,

The rule of Cato, a deadly sword of Caton,

And that which seems to be the appearance of happiness

Was rejected by all great men:

Fabius, Tuberon... and even you, partly,

Become an example for your descendants!

Cleanse your soul and, ready to everything,

Return to nature and say to fate:

"You want to win? - Seek another!"

A man will not bow down to you.

(End of the first fragment)

(Fragments of Two Letters)

Such are the words

Maybe even I can cure this disease,

I wish it would be easier for him,

And good prayers - like medicine.

He lives with a full life, wanting to prolong it,

Cares not about himself:

Useful for the young generation,

He is generosity, not fear of fate.

Another would put an end to all troubles...

But he, considering death a sin,

Is an example to his sons and grandsons

Of how to live and should be able to.

He does not resist unbearable pain,

Does not consider death as a way to overcome it.

Look at him in the eyes and learn,

Greatness of soul and strength of will,

That without hands breaks bricks.

Do not take into account pain - resist death,

Do not take into account death - how to overcome it.

Who is waiting for death - only suffers,

With joy dying...
