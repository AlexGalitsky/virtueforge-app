# Letter XCIX (On the Death of a Son)

Lucillia greets Seneca!

I am re-sending an old letter that Marcus wrote to me... for his own shortcomings.

He lost a child-son then,

And, in grief, he cried out many nights.

And you, with him in a similar position...

Do not console yourself! 

Believe me, and losing a friend is no excuse

To cry for months.

You - rejoice (although it's not the same as his loss),

Pouring tears over the loss.

What's the point, if he's gone and everything was wasted?!

And are you mourning his friendship too?!

Then, forgive me, but you're being ungrateful...

For not being able to hold on to their memory.

Whoever is rejoicing in the present

Is only enjoying material possessions.

Whoever is looking forward to a bright future

Doesn't know that without God, everything is meaningless.

Isn't it true that what's most reliable is the good things that happen?

It's not worth forgetting,

That our future will become our past,

Like autumn leaves withering away.

There are people who behave nobly,

Losing all their family members in a sea of years:

You should approach their loss calmly

Because you've been following them throughout your life.

Look at how fleeting time is:

Whoever has died is only the precursor to your own burden.

One end is inevitable for every human fate.

You're complaining that he was a person?!

The outcome is one thing, but the timing is different,

Whether it's a child or an old man - they all lived for centuries.

May God grant you mercy,

Look at how quickly centuries disappear in dust.

Consider how insignificant our life span is:

Whoever has died is only getting out of your way,

Leaving you with your own burden.

I'm not asking you to muster up strength:

For a strong spirit doesn't need it.

Ripping one's hair over a child's grave... 

Is not suitable for a father.

I don't want you to become insensitive,

There are things that aren't under our control:

The heart weeps with sorrow,

It's not necessary to just cry out for attention.

Some people loudly wail and sigh,

Ready to collapse into their coffin like a rag doll,

Their grief is forgotten by everyone else,

Even when there's no one around to witness it.

But some people are able to feel deeply,

Without imitating others.

They don't cry out for sympathy,

When they're in pain.

Let the mourners wail with sincerity,

For that's why they're invited to weep.

It's not right to cry just for applause,

To be pitied - that's absurd.

Most people won't understand the depth of your sorrow,

Because their understanding is too coarse.

I'm telling you this, so you'll listen:

You shouldn't cry out for attention,

When it's not necessary.

It's not about blaming yourself for bad luck,

The deceased doesn't feel anything,

They only feel pain when they're dying.

Their fears are what we fear most,

And their death is what we dread the most.

...

Are my words falling on deaf ears?

I've punished you... for being impatient,

Because you couldn't resist fate's blows,

You didn't expect them to come soon enough.

Be healthy.
