### Philosophical Context
Seneca's letter to Marullus, written during the time of his son's death, is a classic example of Stoic philosophy. The letter reflects the idea that life is brief and that we should focus on the present rather than being angry about what has happened to us.

### Essence
- The letter serves as a moral and aesthetic critique of human behavior in the face of the loss of a loved one.
- Seneca emphasizes the importance of resignation and balance in life, rather than anger and weeping.
- He urges Marullus to be joyful for being alive rather than weeping over the loss of his son.
