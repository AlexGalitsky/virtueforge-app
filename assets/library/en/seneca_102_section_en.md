Title: The Philosopher - Like Slow Rivers?

---
# The Philosopher - Like Slow Rivers?

A philosopher is not an orator, nor a wordsmith...

He does not polish his words with precision,

But does not rush to correct them either.

Who writes to make his character straight?

His speech is not for the ears alone,

But for the soul.

If only you could hear him speak...

You would be captivated, forgetting the details:

He spoke, and... became more majestic,

A flame of love burned in his eyes!

It's not the one who is highly praised,

But the one who is unappreciated.

The first to take risks is less valuable:

For he has paid everything in full.

A philosopher does not have a flair for elegance,

He owes it to courage, which recognizes himself.

He cannot hit himself with criticism,

Afraid of his own syntax and words.

Why fiddle with them, despite nature's design?

(In this age, beauty is pursued,

But it doesn't understand: language is alien

To the very essence of human life.)

You won't find laziness or dirt in his speech,

Although he takes his words from the heart.

Is it rough? - Yes... The point is there?

Why are words beautiful and easy to say?

The eloquence of Cicero is smooth and refined,

Like a river flowing gently, ever-changing.

Pollio's speeches have abrupt breaks and jumps,

As if phrases came out of a sword fight.

Our Fabian is not lowly, but calm.

Perhaps he lacks sharpness...

It's not in the ornamentation that his worth lies,

But in truth, which has reached its highest point.

Would you rather have Fabian?

I agree with Cicero here...

To stand for one who is entirely free from flaws

And not be a reason to express sorrow and grief.

Let it be Pollio... I agree with him too,

Perhaps God blessed him so that he could at least be third,

In this field, uniqueness should be shown.

Let Livius Tullus write his dialogues...

I'll leave the seat for himself!

Who surrendered to three of them left many behind

(Count the crowd)

Let's say... he was left behind!

Let his courage not be visible in his speeches,

He did not speak with arrogance towards fortune,

Nor did he scold promiscuity shamefully...

Is it always clear when a river is pure?

Perhaps many points are incorrect,

But his speech sounds like a symphony:

He strived to avoid spoiling the applause,

Strived to teach benevolence!

Let his speeches be rough, but rich,

Capable of captivating intelligent youth...

I won't live to say it myself,

But they will say it someday:

"He saw far... from giant shoulders."

Those who didn't see his light were blind,

There are many masters in the details,

And as for the whole... who is like him?

Study Fabian.

Be healthy.
