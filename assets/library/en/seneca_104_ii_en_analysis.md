### Philosophical Context
Letter CII from Seneca is a response to Lucilius, who contemplates the immortality of the soul and the question of recognition. In this letter, Seneca analyzes the concept of recognition and its connection to virtue.

### Essence
- Recognition is not a good in itself but rather praise or judgment.
- Praise is not the primary source of virtue, which stems from individual actions.
- Recognition is the result of voting and cannot be guaranteed for everyone.
- Seneca asserts that recognition is not the primary criterion for assessing human well-being.
