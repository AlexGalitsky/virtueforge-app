# The Letter to SIV (On Changing Places)

Lucillia greets Seneca!

I have been convinced to leave... Of what?

Of the fever. Its attacks are familiar to me.

Do you remember my brother?

He was ill in Achaia and barely managed to board a ship,

Saying that illness lives not in the body,

But in the land itself.

And I, too, told Pauline

That she had taken on herself the burden of life.

Now that we are united with her,

I try not to get sick.

Do you think one can go against great feelings?

I will hold back my breath with my teeth...

For us both, a evening awaits,

Where our guest is death... without choice.

Let us forget about death for now

Until it arrives.

I must follow the dictates of my soul.

But I believe that we must strive to live,

To accomplish something great.

When your wife loves you so much that she is willing...

To love herself too.

Such joy - the thought of death destroys all our thoughts.

Can we think of anything else but not killing our wives?

What was the purpose of my journey?

I returned home and regained my strength,

The illnesses no longer bothered me,

Only the wind rustles my clothes.

Who travels often is wrong,

Whether he has arrived in Athens or Crete,

He sees cities, meets people...

But his own nature remains the same.

If you see wealth as a blessing,

You will find it all lacking,

And if you envy someone who has given money and an award,

You will be consumed by jealousy.

Do you fear death more than you should?

Death is not something to be feared in advance.

Why do we run away from it?

It is not because of the pain, but because of our own fear.

Let us take Socrates as an example,

How many evils befell him,

But he remembered his wife...

And overcame his exhaustion.

Then came his trial for impiety,

And... the most severe sentences:

From hubris to corruption of young men.

He said: "I was what I am, and that is all."

His face did not change until the end.

He endured the injustice of others.

Another example - let us take Catiline!

He lived recently, and there is much to consider,

But he managed to live without looking at obstacles,

And, despite fortune, he died.

He rose and fell with his rank,

But his appearance never changed.

He lived as a man should:

Not "maybe", but "yes" or "no".

The trivialities of life are against him,

While freedom is in the center.

But no matter what circumstances arise,

He was - even if condemned, not defeated.

He had chosen for himself: death and exile,

At first, he chose to fight.

In his courage - in his blood and subconscious,

His fate did not present itself to him.

Freedom is not given to us by chance,

It is not found where silver rules.

The Icarians will be remembered,

Those who valued freedom.

Be healthy.
