### Philosophical Context
Letter CVI is part of Seneca's philosophical work on ethics and virtue. In this letter, Seneca contemplates the nature of goods and whether goods are corporeal.

### Essence
- Goods are seen as what is beneficial to us.
- Corporeal sensations are linked to emotions such as anger, sorrow, and love, as well as the vices they engender: cruelty, stinginess, hypocrisy, and flattery.
- The essence of goods is seen alongside corporeal vision: the brave person is recognized by their bold gaze, composure by their reverence, and peace and mildness by their truthfulness.
- The movement of the body and changes in its properties can influence our perception of goods.

### Form of This Version
Seneca's letter is written in prose.

### Connection to Virtue
The correspondence between goods and corporeal vision is also linked to the connection between virtue and the body. Just as virtue commands the body, so does vice, and what moves the body, we rightly call corporeal.

### Question to Ponder
Does the body constitute a good?
