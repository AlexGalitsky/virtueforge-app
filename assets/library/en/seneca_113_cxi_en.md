**Letter CXI (On Sophisms and Philosophy)**

Lucilia greets Seneca!

Sophism is a product of Greece. Latin.

I could not find a synonym for it that has been used since ancient times:

Sophisms are not for the Romans, but rather for those who dwell in the wilderness!

I think it is acceptable to call them "turning aside" (as Cicero did):

Sophisms "turned aside" or

Characterized from all sides.

Whoever falls prey to them - into trivial matters,

Falls and gains nothing.

But where philosophy travels, there is an unattainable pinnacle.

Whoever seeks healing for himself finds only:

A great burden, a fate that cannot be overcome.

He seems more certain and higher than ever as you approach him.

Standing before towering mountains,

The peak of the cloud is not visible.

Whoever is genuine in his words, but not in his actions,

Will never reach them (the pinnacle).

His greatness is true:

Above all, without high-sounding phrases.

To add growth to oneself by trickery

Is only for those who are driven by self-love.

But he: himself, and his growth, and everything is content with him.

Even more so, if you want to be dissatisfied!

Those who do not notice fortune's wind,

Will never pass through any experience.

And whoever has risen above the crowd,

Cannot approach a person,

Who believes in fate, with his own step:

Accelerates the turn of events.

I will not forbid turning aside:

If you want to waste your time - let us...

But, "blessings enter narrow gates" (and if they do so),

Do not forget them!

Sophisms have a certain pleasantness,

Like palindromes... in hay for cows...

Like a net that catches and does not release.

Learning from contempt for life.

Be healthy.
