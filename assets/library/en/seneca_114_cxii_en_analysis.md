### Philosophical Context
Letter CXII is part of Seneca's cycle in which he speaks to Lucilius about how to help a friend who is too rigid and unwilling to heal. This text reflects the philosophical idea that people often do not want to change and that healing requires readiness and submission to oneself.

### Core Message
- People are often broken by vices and are not ready to heal.
- Sincerity and kindness can be harmful if they are not accompanied by wisdom and knowledge.
- The desire for healing may merely be a desire to avoid the burden or inconvenience.
- Seneca emphasizes the importance of understanding human nature and the necessity of submitting oneself for true healing.

### Form of This Version
Prose.

### Connection to Virtue
Virtue in this text is linked to the idea that Seneca underscores the importance of understanding human nature and the necessity of submitting oneself for true healing. This aligns with the notion that virtue requires a person to be ready for change and submission to oneself.

### Question to Ponder
Is the desire for healing always a genuine desire, or can it be merely a desire to avoid the burden?
