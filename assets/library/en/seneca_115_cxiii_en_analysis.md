### Philosophical Context
Letter CXIII is part of Seneca's philosophical work, where he examines the concept of animating goods and its connection to virtue. This text is a dialogue between Seneca and his friend, in which they discuss the nature of virtue and how it relates to the animation of goods.

### Essence
- Reason and justice are not beings.
- Yet, they possess animation.
- Proper mental exercises are the correct understanding of animation.
- Sensory movements can be viewed as a metabolic exchange.
- Any being, from sensations to impulses and concurrence, possesses a soul.
- The correct form of virtue is the sum of various sentiments based on one soul.
- Justice is a beneficial virtue, but the entities within it lack names.
