### Philosophical Context
Seneca's letter "On the Corruption of Language" is part of his philosophical work aimed at developing moral and ethical principles. In this letter, he criticizes the corruption of language, which he views as a symptom of moral decay.

### Essence
- Seneca argues that the corruption of language is a consequence of moral decay.
- He points out that the corruption of language can stem from various factors, such as ornate style, innovation, and a desire for recognition.
- Seneca emphasizes the importance of restraint in speech and the necessity of monitoring one's thoughts and feelings to avoid moral corruption.

### Connection to Virtue
The corruption of language is directly linked to virtue, as it is a symptom of moral decay. Seneca underscores the importance of cultivating moral and ethical principles for the development of virtue.
