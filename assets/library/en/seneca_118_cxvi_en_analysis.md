### Philosophical Context
Letter CXVI (On Passions) is one of the most renowned works by the Roman philosopher Seneca, written in verse. In it, he addresses Lucilius on the nature of passions and their impact on human beings.

### Essence
* Passions are viewed as a concession to the soul's frailties.
* Seneca asserts that passions should be moderated or rigorously expelled, rather than managed.
* He also emphasizes the importance of recognizing one's vices in actions and the necessity of remaining calm.
* Seneca proposes a model of behavior: to be the master of one's passions, not their slave.

### Form of This Version
Letter CXVI is written in verse.

### Connection to Virtue
Seneca associates passions with vices and underscores the importance of being aware of one's actions to avoid their influence. This aligns with the idea that virtue lies in the ability to control one's passions and choose the right path.

### Question to Ponder
Who among us can truly control their passions?
