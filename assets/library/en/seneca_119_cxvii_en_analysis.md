### Philosophical Context
Letter CXVII by Seneca is a response to Lucilius, who had pondered whether wisdom is a good or not. In this letter, Seneca examines the concept of wisdom and its connection to virtue.

### Core
- Wisdom is not a direct good.
- Being wise does not necessarily equate to being virtuous.
- A sage and wisdom are one and the same, as they both stem from the same nature.
- Wisdom is the ability to govern oneself and make decisions, rather than merely deriving benefit from something.
- Virtue is not necessarily linked to wisdom.

### Form of This Version
Paraphrase: Wisdom is not a direct good, and being wise does not necessarily equate to being virtuous.

### Connection to Virtue
Wisdom and virtue are related, but they are not the same. Virtue involves impacting others and striving for the good, whereas wisdom pertains to personal growth and self-understanding.
