### Philosophical Context
Letter CXXIII, penned by Lucilius to Seneca, embodies a philosophical reflection on the notion that poor guidance and deceit can lead to vice and a downward spiral of well-being. This text reflects Stoic philosophy, emphasizing the importance of restraint, modesty, and resistance to external influences.

### Core Message
- Poor guidance and deceit can lead to vice and a decline in well-being.
- Stoic philosophy underscores the importance of restraint, modesty, and resistance to external pressures.
- We must be cautious around those who attempt to deceive or coerce us into actions we do not wish to undertake.
- A philosopher must be truthful and bold in their counsel.

### Connection to Virtue
Virtue is a central concept in Stoic philosophy. According to the text, modesty and restraint are crucial aspects of virtue, as they enable us to remain free from external influences and maintain our integrity.

### Question for Reflection
Who among us has not been influenced by poor guidance or deceit, and how can we be more cautious around those who seek to deceive us?
