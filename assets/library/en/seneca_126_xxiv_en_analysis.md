### Philosophical Context
This text is a letter from Seneca in which he poses the question: by what means do we apprehend the good? By reason or by sense? This question is a key aspect of Stoicism and underscores the importance of reason and reflection in the pursuit of the good.

### Core
- The essence of the good cannot be grasped solely through the senses; one must also consider the role of reason and reflection.
- The good resides in a soul that is elevated, free, and subject to no one.
- Vice arises when virtue is displaced, and the senses lead to delusion.
- Well-being is the result of perfection, which can be attained through reason and reflection.
